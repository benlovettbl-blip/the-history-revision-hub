Part I: Foundational Pillars — Counsell, Quigley, Hochman & Wexler, Coe & Cognitive Load
Academic Rationale: Every live feature in The History Revision Hub is grounded in peer-reviewed cognitive science and history education scholarship—combining authentic disciplinary enquiry with low-extraneous-load scaffolding across Key Stage 2, Key Stage 3, and Pearson Edexcel GCSE.
🏛️ 1. Christine Counsell: 3-Layer Enquiry & 5-Stage Narrative
Core Concept: Substantive historical facts only endure as residual schema when carried inside a coherent causal narrative answering a genuine enquiry question.

Live Platform Implementation:
  • 5-Stage Narrative Spine ([X.1]–[X.5]): Each lesson unfolds across 5 structured two-paragraph narrative blocks—eliminating disjointed worksheet trivia.
  • Alternating Archival Visual Plates: 2–3 authentic visual plates flank the narrative prose with academic captions and zero clutter.
  • 5 Rotated Primary Sources (Sources A–E): Collapsible drawers rotate decrees, maps, photographs, diaries, and artefacts with natural 1–2 sentence model answers and Hinge Questions (zero formulaic NOP boxes).
References: Counsell, C. (2000). Teaching History, 100 • Dawson, I. (2004). Teaching History, 117.
www.history.org.uk (Historical Association)
📚 2. Alex Quigley: Closing the Vocabulary Gap Without Clutter
Core Concept: Explicit Tier-2/3 vocabulary support unlocks comprehension, provided definitions do not crowd printed reading margins.

Live Platform Implementation:
  • Ambient In-Text Vocabulary Popovers: Dotted underlines (once per root term per lesson) open age-calibrated definitions and spoken audio in place on hover/tap.
  • Inline Dossier Quick-Peek Pills: First mentions of historical actors open contextual [🪪 Person] and [🏛️ Organisation] biographical popovers.
  • Tap-Tile Summary Cloze & Lexical Banks: Every lesson ends with an interactive Consolidate summary cloze (+2 adjacent-lesson decoys) plus Flashcard decks.
References: Quigley, A. (2018). Closing the Vocabulary Gap, Routledge • EEF Secondary Literacy.
www.theconfidentteacher.co.uk
✍️ 3. Hochman, Wexler & Coe: Zero-Giveaway Writing Scaffolds
Core Concept: Writing scaffolds must support sentence structure and genre mechanics without leaking factual answers ("memory is the residue of thought").

Live Platform Implementation:
  • Split [Word Bank ▾] + [Sentence Starters ▾] Toolbar: Digital writing panels separate unassigned Tier-3 terms from structural sentence openers.
  • Content-Free Workbook Connectives: Printed Recto pages provide structural transitions ("One key consequence was that...") with zero factual giveaways.
  • Post-Writing Exemplar Reveal: Complete high-tariff Model Answers and Mark Schemes stay hidden in digital drawers until after independent writing.
References: Hochman & Wexler (2017). The Writing Revolution • Coe et al. (2014). What Makes Great Teaching?
www.thewritingrevolution.org
🧠 4. John Sweller: Cognitive Load & Typographic Ergonomics
Core Concept: Working memory is strictly limited; eliminating visual clutter and spatial split-attention preserves bandwidth for causal reasoning.

Live Platform Implementation:
  • Pure 2-Column Textbooks (Lora Serif): Constrains line length to 50–65 characters with zero sidebars, zero vocab ribbons, and zero in-prose bracket pills.
  • Flat Facing Double-Page Workbooks: Every lesson sits on a single open Verso + Recto spread—never forcing page-flipping to answer questions.
  • Automated Page-Budget Gate: Headless Chromium and pdf-parse audits enforce 0px overflow across all saddle-stitched booklets.
Reference: Sweller, J. (1988; 2019). Cognitive Load Theory • EEF Cognitive Science Review.
educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk

Part II: SEND Accessibility by Design, Spaced Retrieval Isolation & Low-Workload Formative Feedback
♿ 5. SEND & Inclusive Accessibility by Design
Core Concept: High visual predictability and multimodal scaffolding—never dumbed-down content—make rigorous history accessible to neurodiverse learners.

Enforced Across Platform & Print:
  • ✅ Age-Calibrated 2-Paragraph Blocks: Budgeted strictly by cohort (50–80w KS2, 80–110w KS3, 110–140w GCSE) to prevent wall-of-text tracking fatigue.
  • ✅ Persistent Visual-Stress Overlays: Tinted screen overlays restore instantly on load with zero white flash for Irlen/visual-stress readers.
  • ✅ 1-Page Visual Story Sheets: Studio-voiced 6-card (4-card KS2) archival storyboards accompany every single lesson across all units.
  • ✅ ~7.8mm–8.2mm Monochrome Ruled Lines: Generous full-width handwriting pitch supports dysgraphic and fine-motor needs.
References: EEF (2020). SEN in Mainstream Schools • British Dyslexia Association Style Guide.
educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/.../send
🔄 6. Barak Rosenshine: Spaced Recall Isolation & Hinges
Core Concept: Daily retrieval strengthens long-term schema only when starters test previously taught material rather than forcing guesses on untaught topics.

Live Platform Implementation:
  • Zero Self-Testing Rule: Digital Do Now starters are strictly forbidden from testing today's new lesson content.
  • 3-Mode Spaced Retrieval Deck: Toggles cleanly between Last Lesson, Earlier in Unit interleaving, and Synoptic Prior Units with a single-row timer.
  • Targeted Hinge Questions: Embedded at the end of every primary source context and Teacher Note objective to check understanding mid-lesson.
References: Rosenshine, B. (2012). Principles of Instruction • Roediger & Karpicke (2006).
www.aft.org/ae/spring2012/rosenshine
📊 7. Dylan Wiliam: Low-Workload Formative Feedback
Core Concept: Feedback must generate more thinking for the pupil than marking hours for the teacher.

Live Platform Implementation:
  • Self-Marking Lesson Quizzes & Weak Spots: 15–20 traceable MCQs per lesson give immediate feedback and silently log missed items for review.
  • 3-Tier Retrieval Companion Pips (▸ / ▸▸ / ▸▸▸): Printed Quiz Packs ramp from substantive recall to causal explanation and synoptic significance, paired with rear [✓][✗] DIRT Marking Banks.
  • 6-Row Back-Cover Assessment Grid: Tracks core unit enquiries and designated assessments on one scannable back cover.
References: Wiliam, D. (2011). Embedded Formative Assessment • EEF Feedback Guidance.
educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/.../feedback

Part III: Studio Prosody, Anti-Simplification, Guided Reading & Dual-Coding Consolidation
📖 9. Shanahan & Lemov: The Anti-Simplification Standard
Core Concept: Stripping academic vocabulary out of historical texts widens the literacy gap (the Matthew Effect). Pupils progress faster when complex prose is scaffolded rather than simplified.

Why We Reject "Simplify Text" Toggles:
  • ❌ Deleting Tier-2 connectives (consequently, despite, whereas) destroys historical cause-and-effect logic.
  • ❌ Replacing specification nouns with vague everyday synonyms leaves pupils unable to access exam questions or primary sources.
  • ✅ Instead, we calibrate sentence length cleanly while keeping rich vocabulary supported by audio prosody and hover popovers.
References: Shanahan, T. (2025). Leveled Reading, Leveled Lives • Lemov, D. (2016). Reading Reconsidered.
www.shanahanonliteracy.com
🎙️ 10. Built-In Platform Feature: Studio Voices & Guided Reading
Core Concept: Hearing fluent adult prosody while tracking printed text frees working memory from decoding strain.

Built Directly Into the Web App:
  • Multi-Speed Studio Historian Audio (0.85x / 1.0x / 1.15x): Pitch-preserved British documentary narration across every lesson block and Visual Story Sheet with zero-reflow sentence highlighting and click-any-sentence seeking.
  • KS2 & KS3 Guided Reading Companions: Provenance-labelled primary & literary extracts structured as Step 1 (2×2 Key Words) → Step 2 (Studio Read-Aloud) → Step 3 (3-Min Discussion + Answer Guides).
  • GCSE Paper 3 Two-Historian Audio Clashes: Contrasting male/female studio voices bring Interpretation 1 vs 2 debates alive.
References: Quigley, A. (2020). Closing the Reading Gap • Such, C. (2021).
🎯 11. Recommended Teacher Routine: Shared Reading Delivery
Optional Classroom Protocol Alongside Textbook / App:
(Suggested in our 2-Lesson Delivery Plans — teacher-led, not automated):
  1. Pre-Teach 2 Barrier Terms (90s): Preview two Tier-3 words from the lesson Vocabulary Bank before reading begins.
  2. 80/20 Expert Prosody: Teacher (or the 1.0x Studio Player) models 80% of the passage so causal pacing stays brisk.
  3. Spotlight Micro-Turns: Invite developing readers to read a punchy 1–2 sentence conclusion rather than cold round-robin paragraphs.
  4. Pause & Synthesise: Pause at each Stage Question banner for 60–90s of full-sentence Verso note-making.
Reference: Lemov, D. (2021). Teach Like a Champion 3.0 (teachlikeachampion.org).
🗺️ 12. Caviglioli & Novak: Center-Spine Mind-Map Canvas
Core Concept: Combining visual dual-coding with structured causal concept mapping consolidates whole-unit schema without penalizing fine-motor skills.

Live Workbook & App Synergy:
  • Pure #000000 Center-Spine Canvas: Pages 2–3 (or KS3 centrefolds) feature a central chronological spine with specification milestone pills and open white branching space—zero pre-printed summary giveaways.
  • Dual Representation Pathways: KS3 Dual-Pathway timelines let pupils choose between symbolic dual-code icons (Pathway A) or 3-branch causal webs (Pathway B), fuelled by the app's Consolidate Station.
References: Caviglioli, O. (2019). Dual Coding with Teachers • Novak & Gowin (1984).

Part IV: 5-Tier Stage Question Progression, Full-Width Verso Workbooks & Platform Truth Matrix
🪜 13. The 5-Tier Cognitive Question Ladder (KS2 → GCSE)
Core Principle: Every narrative block question (≤ 82 chars single-line fit) scales progressively across age cohorts while remaining 100% answerable from its paired prose block:

  • KS2 (Y5–6) & Year 7: ≤ 13 words, ≤ 2 complex common words — concrete actors, actions, and immediate reactions.
  • Year 8: ≤ 14 words, ≤ 3 complex common words — multi-step causal chains and contrasting perspectives.
  • Year 9: ≤ 15 words, ≤ 4 complex common words — systemic shifts, turning points, and ideology.
  • GCSE (Y10–11): ≤ 15 words, ≤ 5 complex common words — specification-precise mechanisms (open "To what extent..." essays reserved for Recto tasks).
References: Counsell, C. (2000). Historical Knowledge & Historical Skill • Beck, I. et al. (1997). Questioning the Author.
📝 14. Full-Width Verso Boxes & Prose-Hash Sustainability
Eliminating Split-Attention & Curriculum Drift Across All Units:
  • Retired Narrow Trigger Sidebars: Removing cramped 26mm left trigger columns eliminates spatial split-attention and stops pupils copying 2-word fragments, giving 5 full-width ruled lines for complete causal sentences.
  • 1-to-1 App ↔ Print Parity: Identical canonical Stage Questions appear across Web App slim stage banners, Chronology Spine cards, and Printed Pupil Workbooks while preserving noun-phrase milestone titles for Arcade timelines.
  • Automated SHA-1 Prose-Hash Gate: Editing any narrative block automatically enforces stage-question answerability checks during sync:unit and audit:stages.
Reference: Chandler, P. & Sweller, J. (1991). Cognitive Load Theory and the Format of Instruction.
