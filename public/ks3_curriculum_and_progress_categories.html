YEAR 7 HISTORY: Public Health Through Time & Medieval Power (c. 43 AD – 1485)

YEAR 7 HISTORY
In Year 7, pupils are introduced to the core methods of historical enquiry: establishing long-span chronological frameworks, analysing change and continuity across millennia, evaluating contemporary primary sources, and constructing substantiated PEEL paragraphs. Pupils begin in the Autumn Term with a thematic study of Water & Sanitation Through Time before investigating power, religion, and rebellion across Medieval England in the Spring and Summer Terms.
1. YEAR 7 HISTORY Teaching Sequence & Core Enquiries Chronological Delivery Order
Autumn 1 & Autumn 2
1. Water & Sanitation Through Time
Roman aqueducts, bathhouses, and civic engineering; medieval towns, cesspits, and miasma beliefs; Early Modern urban filth; Industrial urbanisation, cholera, laissez-faire attitudes, and the 1858 "Great Stink" (Bazalgette’s London sewers); Dr John Snow’s 1854 Broad Street investigation, Pasteur’s Germ Theory (1861), and the 1848 & 1875 Public Health Acts.
Key Vocabulary: Aqueduct • Cesspit • Miasma • Cholera • Laissez-faire • Germ Theory • Public Health Act  |  Disciplinary Focus: Change & Continuity • Causation • Primary Source Comprehension • PEEL Paragraphs
Spring 1 – Summer 2
2. Medieval England (1066–1485)
1066 & why William of Normandy won at Hastings; Motte-and-bailey castles, the Harrying of the North, feudalism, and the 1086 Domesday Survey; Crown vs Church (Henry II & Thomas Becket); King John, Runnymede, and Magna Carta (1215); the Black Death (1348), Statute of Labourers (1351), and the 1381 Peasants’ Revolt.
Key Vocabulary: Feudalism • Senlac Hill • Domesday Survey • Canon Law • Magna Carta • Clause 39 • Serfdom • Poll Tax  |  Disciplinary Focus: Multi-Causal Explanation • Historical Significance • Contrasting Interpretations • Evaluative Essays
2. YEAR 7 HISTORY Progress Categories & Flight-Path Criteria Aligned with KS3 Pupil Workbooks & Termly Reports
How Progress Categories Work: End-of-term Progress Categories are ‘on track for’ descriptors indicating expected progress across assessments, workbook enquiries, retrieval quizzes, homework, and approach to learning. Learning is not linear, and teachers use these descriptors to identify support or additional challenge so every pupil fulfils their potential.
Progress Category Description Possible Flight Path What This Looks Like in YEAR 7 History
Emerging Working below average national expectations for their age. On track for GCSE grade 1–3 Identifies key historical names, dates, and terms from Year 7 enquiries (such as Roman aqueducts, miasma, 1066, feudalism, or Magna Carta); describes what happened in the past using simple statements and structured writing frames.
Emerging + Working below average national expectations for their age. On track for GCSE grade 3–4 Describes clear reasons why public-health developments and medieval events occurred; begins to explain changes, causes, and consequences supported by basic historical facts and key vocabulary.
Expected Working at average national expectations for their age. On track for GCSE grade 4–5 Constructs structured PEEL paragraphs explaining multiple causes, changes, and consequences (for example, how public health improved in the 19th century or why William won at Hastings); substantiates arguments with precise dates, key terms, and named figures.
Expected + Working at/above average national expectations for their age. On track for GCSE grade 6–7 Weighs competing factors and historical perspectives; explains why certain causes or turning points were more decisive than others (such as science vs government intervention in sanitation, or plague vs political protest in 1381) using accurate evidence across the unit.
Greater depth Working above average national expectations for their age. On track for GCSE grade 8–9 Crafts sustained, analytically rigorous arguments reaching a substantiated historical judgement; synthesizes precise evidence and primary source detail across periods to evaluate non-linear progress, turning points, and long-term significance.

YEAR 8 HISTORY: Reformation, Revolution, Industry & Global Empire (1517–1914)

YEAR 8 HISTORY
In Year 8, pupils investigate how religious upheaval, constitutional revolution, transatlantic trade, industrialisation, and imperial expansion reshaped Britain and the wider world between 1517 and 1914. Pupils develop increasing independence in evaluating primary source utility, contrasting historiographical interpretations, and weighing political, economic, and social factors.
1. YEAR 8 HISTORY Teaching Sequence & Core Enquiries Chronological Delivery Order
Autumn 1
1. Crown, Church, or Parliament? (1517–1689)
Luther, Henry VIII & the Break with Rome; Elizabeth I’s "Middle Way" & the 1588 Spanish Armada; English Civil War, trial & execution of Charles I, and the 1689 Bill of Rights.
Key Vocabulary: Reformation • Supremacy • Armada • Divine Right • Regicide • Constitutional Monarchy  |  Disciplinary Focus: Causation & Motivation • Source Utility • Turning Points
Autumn 2
2. The First British Empire (1600–1783)
East India Company & Mughal Bengal; the Transatlantic Slave Trade & Middle Passage; plantation economies & Maroon resistance (Queen Nanny); the American Revolution (1776).
Key Vocabulary: Charter • Diwani • Middle Passage • Chattel Slavery • Maroons • Black Loyalists  |  Disciplinary Focus: Consequence • Source Provenance • Historical Agency
Spring 1
3. Industrialisation, Empire & Power (1750–1900)
Steam power & local ironworking innovation at Funtley (Henry Cort); factory conditions; industrial towns; Chartism & expansion of parliamentary democracy.
Key Vocabulary: Puddling Process • Urbanisation • Factory Acts • Chartism • Franchise  |  Disciplinary Focus: Change & Continuity • Local-to-Global Significance
Spring 2
4. The Second British Empire (1783–1914)
Royal Navy hegemony; de-industrialisation of India; the 1857 Indian Rebellion (Rani Lakshmibai) & Crown Raj; the Scramble for Africa.
Key Vocabulary: Mercantilism • Sepoy • British Raj • Scramble for Africa • Imperialism  |  Disciplinary Focus: Historiographical Interpretations • Comparative Judgement
Summer 1 & 2
5. The History of Australia (1770–1901)
First Nations custodianship; Captain Cook, Tupaia & the myth of Terra Nullius; 1788 First Fleet penal colony; frontier warfare & Pemulwuy; 1851 Gold Rush & Eureka Stockade.
Key Vocabulary: First Nations • Terra Nullius • Transportation • Frontier Wars • Eureka Stockade  |  Disciplinary Focus: Source Perspective • Causation • Evaluative Statement Essays

How Progress Categories Work: End-of-term Progress Categories are ‘on track for’ descriptors indicating expected progress across assessments, workbook enquiries, retrieval quizzes, homework, and approach to learning. Emerging + Working below average national expectations for their age. On track for GCSE grade 3–4 Describes reasons why major developments occurred (such as the Break with Rome, factory growth, or imperial expansion); begins to explain causes and consequences supported by relevant historical facts.

YEAR 9 HISTORY: Total War, Ideology, Genocide & Modern Rights (1870–Present)

YEAR 9 HISTORY
In Year 9, pupils examine the defining conflicts, ideological clashes, human rights struggles, and social transformations of the modern world. Enquiries build rigorous fluency in primary source utility, causal narrative analysis, and historiographical debate, providing an ambitious disciplinary bridge into Key Stage 4.
1. YEAR 9 HISTORY Teaching Sequence & Core Enquiries Chronological Delivery Order
Autumn 1
1. Causes of the Great War (1870–1914)
German unification & Bismarck; Scramble for Africa & Moroccan Crises; Anglo-German naval arms race (HMS Dreadnought); Triple Alliance vs Triple Entente; Balkan crises & the July 1914 Sarajevo assassination.
Key Vocabulary: Militarism • Weltpolitik • Dreadnought • Encirclement • Blank Cheque • Schlieffen Plan  |  Disciplinary Focus: Long- vs Short-Term Causation • Analytical Narrative
Autumn 2
2. The Great War: Western Front & Aftermath (1914–1919)
Enlistment & conscription; trench warfare, the Somme (1916) & the Haig debate; Empire soldiers on global fronts; women & the Home Front; the 1919 Treaty of Versailles.
Key Vocabulary: Attrition • Artillery Barrage • Western Front • Munitions • Armistice • Diktat  |  Disciplinary Focus: Historiographical Debate • Primary Source Utility
Spring 1 & 2
3. The Shoah & World War II (1933–1948)
Pre-war European Jewish life; Nazi persecution, Nuremberg Laws & Kristallnacht (1933–39); ghettoisation; Operation Barbarossa & the "Holocaust by Bullets"; Auschwitz-Birkenau, resistance, complicity, rescue & liberation.
Key Vocabulary: Antisemitism • Nuremberg Laws • Kristallnacht • Ghetto • Einsatzgruppen • Upstander  |  Disciplinary Focus: Evidential Empathy • Moral & Historical Significance
Summer 1
4. The Cold War & Global Flashpoints (1945–1991)
Capitalism vs Communism; Yalta & Potsdam (1945); Iron Curtain & Berlin Blockade (1948–49); Cuban Missile Crisis (1962); proxy wars in Korea & Vietnam; fall of the Berlin Wall (1989).
Key Vocabulary: Ideology • Containment • Blockade • Brinkmanship • Deterrence • Détente  |  Disciplinary Focus: Consequence Analysis • Multi-Perspective Evaluation
Summer 2
5. Rights, Protest & Post-War Britain (1900–Present)
Suffragists vs Suffragettes; 1945 reconstruction, Beveridge Report, Welfare State & NHS (1948); the Windrush generation; the 1963 Bristol Bus Boycott & British civil rights.
Key Vocabulary: Franchise • Suffragette • Welfare State • Windrush • Boycott • Race Relations Act  |  Disciplinary Focus: Thematic Change & Continuity • Substantiated Judgement
