Enquiry 1: Ice Age Hunters & Sunken Doggerland (c. 800,000–4000 BC)
Q1. Where in Norfolk did archaeologists find 850,000-year-old human footprints pressed into ancient mud? [ Happisburgh / Londinium ]
Q2. What do archaeologists call the Stone Age skill of striking flint rocks to chip off sharp cutting flakes?
Q3. Where was the 10,000-year-old Mesolithic skeleton known as 'Cheddar Man' discovered?
Q4. What remarkable 11,000-year-old Mesolithic objects did archaeologists dig up from wet peat at Star Carr in Yorkshire?
Enquiry 2: The First Farmers & Skara Brae (c. 4000–2500 BC)
Q5. Around 4000 BC, how did the first sheep, cattle, and wheat seeds reach Britain to start Neolithic farming? [ Pioneers carried them across the sea in wooden boats / Families walked them across dry Doggerland ]
Q6. Why did Neolithic craftworkers grind and polish stone axeheads smooth on wet sandstone?
Q7. How was the 5,000-year-old Neolithic village of Skara Brae on Orkney rediscovered in 1850?
Q8. Which surprising features still survive inside the Neolithic houses at Skara Brae?
Enquiry 3: Secrets of Stonehenge (c. 3000–2200 BC)
Q9. What natural tools did Neolithic workers use around 3000 BC to dig Stonehenge’s circular chalk ditch? [ Red-deer antler picks and bone shovels / Iron mechanical diggers ]
Q10. From which mountains 140 miles away in Wales did Neolithic teams haul Stonehenge’s eighty spotted bluestones?
Q11. How did Neolithic carpenters and masons lock horizontal lintel stones onto the top of giant sarsen trilithons?
Q12. What did archaeologists discover at Durrington Walls, just two miles from Stonehenge?

Enquiry 4: Enter the Bronze Age & Beaker People (c. 2500–1200 BC)
Q13. Why do archaeologists call the newcomers who arrived in Britain from Europe around 2400 BC the "Beaker people"? [ They brought tall, bell-shaped clay drinking pots stamped with neat patterns / They built glass science beakers inside laboratories ]
Q14. Where in Britain did Early Bronze Age miners dig copper tunnels and gather tin ore?
Q15. How did Bronze Age smiths make tough golden bronze, and why did it beat chipped flint?
Q16. What were the Mold Gold Cape, gold lunulae, and the Bush Barrow lozenge?
Enquiry 5: Must Farm — "Britain's Pompeii" (c. 1200–800 BC)
Q17. How were the Late Bronze Age roundhouses at Must Farm in Cambridgeshire built around 900 BC? [ High up on sturdy wooden oak stilts driven into a slow-moving river / Deep inside underground chalk caves ]
Q18. Why did wooden spoons, woven willow eel traps, and fine plant-fibre cloth survive for 3,000 years at Must Farm?
Q19. Which record-breaking transport object from around 900 BC was excavated intact from the mud at Must Farm?
Q20. How did Bronze Age shipwrights fasten the thick oak planks of the Dover Bronze Age Boat together?
Enquiry 6: Iron Age Celts & Giant Hillforts (c. 800 BC–AD 43)
Q21. Around 800 BC, why did iron tools spread much more widely across British farms than bronze tools had? [ Reddish iron ore rock was common across Britain, and hammered iron held an even tougher edge than bronze / Iron fell ready-made out of rainclouds every winter ]
Q22. How were Iron Age roundhouses built, and how did families grind grain into flour inside them?
Q23. What was Maiden Castle in Dorset, and how did its giant chalk ramparts protect a Celtic tribe?
Q24. What was the Snettisham Great Torc (c. 100 BC) discovered in Norfolk?
