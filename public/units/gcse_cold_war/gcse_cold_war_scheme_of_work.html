KT 1.1

The Grand Alliance & Wartime Conferences, 1941–45

Central Enquiry: Why did the wartime Grand Alliance unravel between Tehran (1943) and Potsdam (1945)?
Curriculum / Spec Anchor: Key Topic 1.1: The origins of the Cold War, 1941–45 — ideological differences (capitalism vs communism), the Grand Alliance, outcomes of Tehran (1943), Yalta (Feb…
Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This opening GCSE Option P4 enquiry traces how the Grand Alliance shifted from pragmatic wartime compromise at Tehran (Nov 1943) and Yalta (Feb 1945) to open suspicion at Potsdam (July–Aug 1945). Emphasise the three structural hinges that changed between Yalta and Potsdam: (1) defeat of the shared Nazi enemy in May 1945, (2) replacement of conciliatory Roosevelt with anti-communist Truman (and Churchill with Attlee), and (3) the 16 July 1945 US atomic test.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Contrast the ideological foundations of Western capitalism and Soviet communism within the Grand Alliance.
  • Compare the outcomes and shifting power balance across Tehran (1943), Yalta (1945), and Potsdam (1945).
  • Evaluate the impact of Truman’s succession and the atomic bomb on US–Soviet trust in July–August 1945.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This final enquiry of Paper 2 Option P4 connects the 1989 collapse of the Eastern Bloc (Sinatra Doctrine -> Poland/Hungary -> 9 Nov 1989 Berlin Wall -> Velvet Revolution -> Oct 1990 German reunification -> July 1991 Warsaw Pact dissolution) to the internal breakup of the USSR itself (Baltic independence -> Aug 1991 Gang of Eight coup -> Boris Yeltsin -> Dec 1991 CIS -> 25 Dec 1991 Gorbachev resignation).

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain why abandoning the Brezhnev Doctrine ("Sinatra Doctrine") caused rapid regime collapse across Eastern Europe in 1989.
  • Analyse the causes and significance of the fall of the Berlin Wall (9 November 1989) and dissolution of the Warsaw Pact (July 1991).
  • Evaluate how nationalism, the August 1991 coup, and Boris Yeltsin brought down Gorbachev and dissolved the USSR in December 1991.
