Stagnation, Afghanistan & SDI

25% of Soviet Wealth Spent on Arms + Afghan War

1985–1988

Glasnost & Sinatra Doctrine

Gorbachev Ends Red Army Intervention in Europe

Taking office in 1985, Mikhail Gorbachev launched Glasnost (openness) and Perestroika (economic reform), while his “Sinatra Doctrine” let Eastern Europe choose its own path.

9 Nov 1989: Opening the Gates

Crowds Breach Berlin Wall; Germany Unites 1990

After Poland’s Solidarity won elections and Hungary opened its border, spokesman Günter Schabowski’s live TV slip on 9 November 1989 sent crowds surging to tear down the Wall.

Failed Coup & Soviet Collapse

15 Soviet Republics Break Away as USSR Dissolves

When communist hardliners staged an August 1991 coup, Boris Yeltsin rallied crowds atop a Moscow tank. Fifteen republics broke away, and Gorbachev resigned on 25 December 1991.

25 Dec 1991

The Red Flag Comes Down

Germany Reunited (1990) & Cold War Ended (1991)

Drained by economic stagnation and Afghanistan, Gorbachev’s reforms unleashed peaceful 1989 revolutions, reunited Germany in 1990, and ended the Soviet superpower in 1991.

💡 The Big Story in One Sentence

Economic stagnation, the Afghan war, and Gorbachev’s reforms sparked peaceful 1989 revolutions that toppled the Berlin Wall and dissolved the USSR in 1991.

1. Glasnost Gorbachev’s policy of openness allowing freer speech, news, and public debate.
2. Perestroika Gorbachev’s economic restructuring allowing limited private small businesses.
3. Solidarity Polish free trade union led by Lech Wałęsa that won June 1989 elections.
