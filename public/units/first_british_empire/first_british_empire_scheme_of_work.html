Non-Specialist Executive Summary & Core Threads

This Year 8 unit examines the rise, human cost, and fracture of the First British Empire between 1600 and 1783. Pupils investigate how royal charters empowered private corporations—the East India Company in Mughal Bengal and the Royal African Company in West Africa—to build fortunes through military bribery and chattel slavery…

Disciplinary Threads: Multi-Causal Explanation, Primary Source Forensic & Provenance Analysis, Historical Interpretations, and Agency vs Structure.

This enquiry introduces students to the corporate roots of British rule in India. Students evaluate the transformation of the East India Company from an early modern trading venture into a territorial sovereign power, examining the roles of the 1600 charter, Mughal commercial factories, Clive’s opportunistic bribery at Plassey, and the constitutional significance of the 1765 Diwani.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain why the East India Company was formed and how factories operated in Mughal India.
  • Analyse the causal factors that enabled Robert Clive to triumph at the Battle of Plassey.
  • Evaluate the constitutional and human consequences of the 1765 Treaty of Allahabad.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry addresses the economic structure and human trauma of the Transatlantic Slave Trade. Students investigate how manufactured goods, captive human beings, and plantation commodities formed the Triangular Trade, scrutinise forensic evidence from the Brookes diagram, and evaluate how captive resistance and Equiano’s firsthand testimony exposed the brutality of chattel slavery.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain the three legs of the Triangular Trade and identify the goods exchanged on each leg.
  • Evaluate the physical conditions and human suffering endured during the Middle Passage.
  • Analyse how enslaved Africans resisted captivity, from shipboard insurrections to Equiano’s writing.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry challenges the misconception that enslaved Africans were passive victims of empire. Students explore the industrial brutality of Caribbean sugar plantations, examine how daily cultural survival and sabotage undermined planter authority, and analyse how Queen Nanny and the Jamaican Maroons used terrain, camouflage, and guerrilla tactics to force the British Crown into signing a formal peace treaty in 1739.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Describe the organisation and physical brutality of the sugar plantation gang system.
  • Identify and categorise different methods of everyday enslaved resistance.
  • Analyse how Queen Nanny and the Maroons forced the British Crown into the 1739 Peace Treaty.

Students analyse how British national debt prompted Parliament to tax colonial commerce, explore the constitutional friction behind "No taxation without representation", investigate Paul Revere’s propaganda after the Boston Massacre, and evaluate the economic and political catalysts behind the 1773 Boston Tea Party.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain why the Seven Years’ War created imperial debt and prompted new colonial taxation.
  • Analyse the ideological argument behind "No taxation without representation" during the Stamp Act crisis.
  • Deconstruct Paul Revere’s Boston Massacre engraving as a piece of political propaganda.
