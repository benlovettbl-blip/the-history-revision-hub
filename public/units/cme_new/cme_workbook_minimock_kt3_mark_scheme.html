Page 10 • Question 1: Explain Two Consequences [8 Marks — 2 × 4m] Mark Each Consequence Independently out of 4
Explain two consequences of US involvement in the Gulf War (1991) for the Middle East peace process. [8 marks]
Pearson Edexcel Paper 2 Q1 Marking Rule: Mark Consequence 1 (0–4 marks) and Consequence 2 (0–4 marks) separately. Level 1 (1–2m): Simple/general statement • Level 2 (3–4m): Specific factual detail + explained causal outcome.
Consequence 1 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of US involvement in the 1990–91 Gulf War was the financial and diplomatic isolation of Yasser Arafat and the PLO, pushing them to compromise in peace talks. Because Arafat backed Iraqi leader Saddam Hussein against the US-led coalition, wealthy Gulf Arab states cut off financial subsidies to the PLO and expelled Palestinian workers. Bankrupt and weakened after also losing Soviet patronage, Arafat needed diplomatic breakthroughs at Madrid (1991) and Oslo (1993).

Consequence 2 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

A second consequence was unmatched American diplomatic authority across the Middle East that enabled Washington to convene the October 1991 Madrid Peace Conference. Having led a victorious international coalition and emerged from the Cold War as the sole superpower, President George H. W. Bush and Secretary of State James Baker brought Israeli, Syrian, Lebanese, and Jordanian-Palestinian delegations into direct face-to-face bilateral negotiations for the first time.

Page 11 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks] (Unscaffolded) 8 Marks (AO1: 4 + AO2: 4)
Explain the importance of changing superpower policies and the end of the Cold War for attempts to find a solution in the Middle East. [8 marks]
Level 3 (6–8 Marks): Two distinct, well-evidenced analytical paragraphs consistently explaining why the first factor mattered specifically for the second part of the question stem.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991 ended decades of Cold War superpower rivalry in which Moscow had armed and shielded Arab confrontation states and the PLO against compromise. Deprived of their superpower arms patron and Soviet diplomatic backing — while Arafat also lost Gulf Arab funding after supporting Saddam Hussein in the 1990–91 Gulf War — Syria and the PLO could no longer pursue a military option against Israel and had to enter American-led peace negotiations.

Second, the end of the Cold War left the United States as the sole global superpower able to sponsor face-to-face regional diplomacy without Soviet obstruction. Working alongside Moscow after the 1991 Gulf War victory, President George H. W. Bush and James Baker convened the October 1991 Madrid Peace Conference breaking historic taboos against direct talks, while President Bill Clinton’s administration later hosted and underwrote the September 1993 Oslo I Accord handshake and the October 1994 Israel-Jordan Peace Treaty.
