Page 14 • Question 1: Explain Two Consequences [8 Marks — 2 × 4m] Mark Each Consequence Independently out of 4
Explain two consequences of the PFLP airplane hijacks of 1970. [8 marks]
Pearson Edexcel Paper 2 Q1 Marking Rule: Mark Consequence 1 (0–4 marks) and Consequence 2 (0–4 marks) separately. Level 1 (1–2m): Simple/general statement • Level 2 (3–4m): Specific factual detail + explained causal outcome.
Consequence 1 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the PFLP airplane hijacks of 6 September 1970 was King Hussein’s military crackdown and expulsion of the PLO from Jordan in Black September. By landing three hijacked Western passenger jets at Dawson’s Field in Jordan and blowing them up on world television in defiance of royal authority, George Habash’s PFLP convinced Hussein his throne was in danger. On 16 September 1970, Jordanian tanks shelled guerrilla camps in Amman, driving the PLO out to southern Lebanon by 1971.

Consequence 2 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

A second consequence was worldwide television publicity for the Palestinian cause combined with tougher international counter-terrorism security. Holding over 300 civilian hostages forced Western governments to negotiate prisoner releases and thrust Palestinian grievances onto global news broadcasts. At the same time, hijacking civilian airliners alienated Western governments and reinforced American backing for King Hussein and Israel against militant Palestinian factions.

Page 15 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks] (Unscaffolded) 8 Marks (AO1: 4 + AO2: 4)
Explain the importance of Israel’s consolidation of control of the occupied territories for Arab-Israeli relations in the years 1967–73. [8 marks]
Level 3 (6–8 Marks): Two distinct, well-evidenced analytical paragraphs consistently explaining why the first factor mattered specifically for the second part of the question stem.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, Israel’s consolidation of military and civilian control over the lands captured in June 1967 entrenched diplomatic deadlock with Egypt and Syria and helped cause the 1973 Yom Kippur War. By formally annexing East Jerusalem, building the fortified Bar-Lev Line along the blocked Suez Canal, and establishing Allon Plan outposts and West Bank settlements while rejecting Sadat’s 1971 Sinai peace offer, Israel convinced Arab states that UN Resolution 242 would never be implemented peacefully. Consequently, Egypt and Syria launched both the 1969–70 War of Attrition and the surprise attack of 6 October 1973 to break Israel’s grip by force.

Second, military occupation over more than one million Palestinians alongside 300,000 new refugees fuelled militant Palestinian resistance that destabilised the wider region. With Palestinians denied self-determination under Israeli rule, recruits poured into Fatah and the PFLP after the 1968 Battle of Karameh, leading to the 1970 Dawson’s Field hijackings, Jordan’s Black September civil war, and the 1972 Munich Olympics massacre. Israeli air strikes and Operation Wrath of God ensured the Palestinian issue remained an explosive barrier to regional peace.
