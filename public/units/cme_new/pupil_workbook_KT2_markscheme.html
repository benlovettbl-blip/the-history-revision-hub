Page 3 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of Israel’s raid on Samu in November 1966. [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of Israel’s massive reprisal raid on Samu in the Jordanian West Bank on 13 November 1966 was violent anti-government rioting that destabilised King Hussein’s monarchy. Because 4,000 IDF soldiers supported by tanks flattened over 100 houses and killed 15 Jordanian soldiers in retaliation for a Fatah landmine, West Bank Palestinians rioted accusing Hussein of failing to defend them. To survive politically, King Hussein had to abandon secret moderation and harden his public stance toward Israel.

Page 3 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Explain the importance of the Cairo Conference (1964) and the growth of Fatah for escalating tension in the Middle East in the years 1964–67. [8 marks]
Edexcel Q3 Importance Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Generalised assertions • Level 2 (3–5m): Descriptive account with limited link to importance • Level 3 (6–8m): Two distinct, factually supported analytical paragraphs directly explaining why X mattered for Y.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, the January 1964 Arab League Summit in Cairo turned water disputes and Palestinian nationalism into coordinated Arab policy against Israel. Alarmed by Israel’s completion of the National Water Carrier pumping water from the Sea of Galilee to the Negev, Arab leaders launched the Headwater Diversion Plan in Syria and Jordan and established the Palestine Liberation Organisation (PLO) committed to armed struggle. Between 1965 and 1966, Israeli tank and air strikes bombed Syrian earth-moving equipment, making armed border clashes routine.

Second, the growth of Yasser Arafat’s Fatah movement drove a relentless cycle of guerrilla sabotage and massive Israeli reprisals that destabilised Israel’s neighbours. Launching raids from January 1965 and backed from 1966 by Syria’s radical military regime alongside Golan Heights artillery shelling, Fatah landmines provoked Israel’s November 1966 assault on Samu in Jordan and contributed to the 7 April 1967 dogfight in which Israeli Mirage jets shot down six Syrian MiG-21s over Damascus — pushing Syria to demand Egyptian military action.

Enquiry Question (Page 4): How did superpower miscalculation and Nasser’s brinkmanship trigger Israel’s Six Day War victory?
Page 5 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the events of 7 April 1967. [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the aerial battle of 7 April 1967 was military humiliation for Syria that drew the Soviet Union and Egypt into the May 1967 crisis. Because Israeli Mirage jets shot down six Syrian MiG-21 fighters without loss and chased planes over Damascus, Syrian leaders pressed Cairo for help under their defence pact. Fearing for its Syrian ally, the Soviet Union passed a false intelligence warning to Nasser on 13 May claiming Israeli brigades were massing on Syria’s border, prompting Egypt to mobilise 100,000 troops into Sinai.

Page 5 • Question 2: Narrative Account [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Write a narrative account analysing the key events leading up to and during the Six Day War in May–June 1967. [8 marks]
Stimulus bullets printed: • The closure of the Straits of Tiran (22 May 1967) • Israel’s pre-emptive air strike (5 June 1967)
Edexcel Q2 Narrative Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Simple statements • Level 2 (3–5m): Chronological description with implicit links • Level 3 (6–8m): Organised beginning–middle–outcome sequence with explicit causal links & own knowledge beyond the two stimulus points.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

On 13 May 1967, a false Soviet intelligence warning alleging an Israeli troop buildup against Syria prompted Egyptian President Nasser to march 100,000 troops into Sinai and expel UNEF peacekeepers on 16 May. Escalating further, Nasser closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping on 22 May and signed a Joint Defence Pact with Jordan’s King Hussein on 30 May; when US President Lyndon B. Johnson failed to reopen Tiran by sea, Israel appointed Moshe Dayan Defence Minister to prepare a pre-emptive strike.

At 07:45 on 5 June 1967, the Israeli Air Force launched Operation Focus, flying beneath radar to crater Egyptian runways and destroy over 300 Egyptian aircraft on the ground within three hours. Shielded by total air supremacy between 5 and 8 June, Israeli armoured divisions under Tal, Yoffe, and Sharon routed Egyptian forces through the Mitla Pass to the Suez Canal, while Jordanian shelling of West Jerusalem triggered an Israeli counter-attack that captured East Jerusalem’s Old City and the West Bank by 7 June.

With Egypt and Jordan defeated, Dayan ordered a final assault on 9 June up the fortified Syrian Golan Heights, seizing Quneitra before a UN Security Council ceasefire took effect on 10 June 1967. In six days Israel tripled the territory under its control—occupying Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and the Golan Heights—placing over one million Palestinians under military occupation.

Enquiry Question (Page 6): Why did the 1967 territorial conquests and UN Resolution 242 produce deadlock rather than peace?
Page 7 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the continued dispute over the Suez Canal after June 1967. [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the continued dispute over the Suez Canal after June 1967 was the prolonged closure of the waterway and the outbreak of the War of Attrition (1969–70). Because the Suez Canal remained blocked by sunken wrecks while Israel constructed the 20-metre sand ramparts and bunkers of the Bar-Lev Line along the east bank, Egypt lost vital canal revenues and feared Sinai’s occupation becoming permanent. From March 1969, Nasser launched heavy artillery bombardments and commando raids across the canal to wear down Israeli resolve.

Page 7 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Explain the importance of UN Resolution 242 (November 1967) for Arab-Israeli relations after the Six Day War. [8 marks]
Edexcel Q3 Importance Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Generalised assertions • Level 2 (3–5m): Descriptive account with limited link to importance • Level 3 (6–8m): Two distinct, factually supported analytical paragraphs directly explaining why X mattered for Y.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, UN Security Council Resolution 242 (22 November 1967) was important because it established the enduring "Land for Peace" diplomatic framework for future Middle East negotiations. Passed unanimously after Arab leaders issued the Khartoum Summit’s uncompromising "Three Noes" (1 September 1967), British-drafted Resolution 242 linked Israeli military withdrawal from conquered territory to Arab recognition of every regional state’s right to live in peace within secure and recognised borders, creating the benchmark later used in Egyptian, Jordanian, and Palestinian peace talks.

Second, deliberate ambiguity in Resolution 242’s wording entrenched diplomatic deadlock with Arab states and alienated the PLO. Calling in English for withdrawal from "territories occupied" rather than "the territories" allowed Israel to argue for defensible border adjustments while building the Bar-Lev Line and Allon Plan settlements, whereas Arab governments demanded total pre-war withdrawal; meanwhile, referring to Palestinians merely as a "refugee problem" ignored Palestinian statehood, prompting the PLO to reject Resolution 242 and escalate armed struggle.

Enquiry Question (Page 10): How did PLO militancy, expulsion from Jordan, and Munich shape international attitudes to Palestine?
Page 11 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the expulsion of the PLO from Jordan (1970). [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of King Hussein’s expulsion of the PLO from Jordan during Black September (1970–71) was the relocation of the PLO’s political leadership and thousands of armed fighters to Beirut and southern Lebanon. Driven out of Jordan after tank battles in Amman claimed thousands of lives, the PLO established a new armed base in Lebanese refugee camps while forming the underground Black September faction to avenge its defeat. This shifted cross-border raids and Israeli retaliation onto Israel’s northern border with Lebanon.

Page 11 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Explain the importance of Black September and the Munich Olympics (1972) for international attitudes towards the Palestine issue. [8 marks]
Edexcel Q3 Importance Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Generalised assertions • Level 2 (3–5m): Descriptive account with limited link to importance • Level 3 (6–8m): Two distinct, factually supported analytical paragraphs directly explaining why X mattered for Y.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, the Black September attack at the Munich Olympics on 5 September 1972 forced the Palestinian national struggle onto global television screens, making it impossible for world leaders to ignore Palestinian statelessness. When eight militants stormed the Olympic Village demanding the release of over 200 Palestinian prisoners — killing two Israelis immediately before all nine hostages died in a botched German rescue at Fürstenfeldbruck airfield — hundreds of millions of viewers saw that Palestinians demanded national recognition rather than refugee charity alone.

Second, however, killing 11 Israeli athletes and coaches provoked fierce international condemnation of terrorism and hardened Western and Israeli refusal to negotiate with the PLO. World governments tightened airport and Olympic security and backed Prime Minister Golda Meir’s air strikes and Mossad’s covert Operation Wrath of God assassinations across Europe and Beirut. Seeing that overseas terror tarnished Palestinian legitimacy, Yasser Arafat increasingly looked toward winning diplomatic recognition at the United Nations by 1974.

Key Topic 2.5: War of Attrition, Sadat’s Diplomacy & The Yom Kippur War (1969–73)
Enquiry Question (Page 12): Why did Sadat launch the 1973 Yom Kippur War, and how did its aftermath reshape diplomacy?
Page 13 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the Yom Kippur War (1973). [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the Yom Kippur War (October 1973) was the shattering of the myth of Israeli military invulnerability, creating the conditions for US-led peace talks. Because Egyptian troops breached the Bar-Lev Line and Syrian tanks broke into the Golan Heights while inflicting heavy Israeli losses, Anwar Sadat restored Arab pride even after Israel counter-crossed the Suez Canal. Combined with OPEC’s oil embargo and UN Resolution 338, this shock forced Israel and the USA into serious negotiations.

Page 13 • Question 2: Narrative Account [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Write a narrative account analysing Egyptian relations with Israel, the USA and the USSR in the years 1969–73. [8 marks]
Stimulus bullets printed: • The War of Attrition (1969–70) • The Yom Kippur War (October 1973)
Edexcel Q2 Narrative Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Simple statements • Level 2 (3–5m): Chronological description with implicit links • Level 3 (6–8m): Organised beginning–middle–outcome sequence with explicit causal links & own knowledge beyond the two stimulus points.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

In March 1969, Nasser launched the War of Attrition along the Suez Canal to prevent the 1967 ceasefire line becoming permanent, trading artillery fire across Israel’s Bar-Lev Line. When Israeli jets bombed deep inside Egypt, Moscow deployed surface-to-air missiles and Soviet pilots to defend Cairo, raising fears of superpower conflict that prompted a US-brokered ceasefire in August 1970.

After Anwar Sadat succeeded Nasser in September 1970, Golda Meir and Washington ignored his 1971 offer to reopen Suez in return for Sinai withdrawal, while Moscow withheld offensive strike aircraft during Cold War détente. Consequently, Sadat expelled 15,000 Soviet military advisers in July 1972 to show Washington Egypt’s independence, while coordinating a limited war with Syria using Soviet anti-tank and anti-aircraft missiles to break the diplomatic freeze.

On 6 October 1973 (Yom Kippur), Egyptian troops crossed the Suez Canal using high-pressure water cannon as Syria attacked the Golan Heights. Heavy early IDF losses prompted Nixon’s Operation Nickel Grass airlift on 14 October to match Soviet flights, enabling Ariel Sharon to cross the canal and encircle Egypt’s Third Army; after Soviet intervention threats triggered a brief US nuclear alert alongside OPEC’s 17 October oil embargo, Washington and Moscow enforced UN Resolution 338, pivoting Sadat toward US mediation.

Explain two consequences of the PFLP airplane hijacks of 1970. [8 marks]
Pearson Edexcel Paper 2 Q1 Marking Rule: Mark Consequence 1 (0–4 marks) and Consequence 2 (0–4 marks) separately. Level 1 (1–2m): Simple/general statement • Level 2 (3–4m): Specific factual detail + explained causal outcome.
Consequence 1 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the PFLP airplane hijacks of 6 September 1970 was King Hussein’s military crackdown and expulsion of the PLO from Jordan in Black September. By landing three hijacked Western passenger jets at Dawson’s Field in Jordan and blowing them up on world television in defiance of royal authority, George Habash’s PFLP convinced Hussein his throne was in danger. On 16 September 1970, Jordanian tanks shelled guerrilla camps in Amman, driving the PLO out to southern Lebanon by 1971.

Consequence 2 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

A second consequence was worldwide television publicity for the Palestinian cause combined with tougher international counter-terrorism security. Holding over 300 civilian hostages forced Western governments to negotiate prisoner releases and thrust Palestinian grievances onto global news broadcasts. At the same time, hijacking civilian airliners alienated Western governments and reinforced American backing for King Hussein and Israel against militant Palestinian factions.

Page 15 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks] (Unscaffolded) 8 Marks (AO1: 4 + AO2: 4)
Explain the importance of Israel’s consolidation of control of the occupied territories for Arab-Israeli relations in the years 1967–73. [8 marks]
Level 3 (6–8 Marks): Two distinct, well-evidenced analytical paragraphs consistently explaining why the first factor mattered specifically for the second part of the question stem.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, Israel’s consolidation of military and civilian control over the lands captured in June 1967 entrenched diplomatic deadlock with Egypt and Syria and helped cause the 1973 Yom Kippur War. By formally annexing East Jerusalem, building the fortified Bar-Lev Line along the blocked Suez Canal, and establishing Allon Plan outposts and West Bank settlements while rejecting Sadat’s 1971 Sinai peace offer, Israel convinced Arab states that UN Resolution 242 would never be implemented peacefully. Consequently, Egypt and Syria launched both the 1969–70 War of Attrition and the surprise attack of 6 October 1973 to break Israel’s grip by force.

Second, military occupation over more than one million Palestinians alongside 300,000 new refugees fuelled militant Palestinian resistance that destabilised the wider region. With Palestinians denied self-determination under Israeli rule, recruits poured into Fatah and the PFLP after the 1968 Battle of Karameh, leading to the 1970 Dawson’s Field hijackings, Jordan’s Black September civil war, and the 1972 Munich Olympics massacre. Israeli air strikes and Operation Wrath of God ensured the Palestinian issue remained an explosive barrier to regional peace.
