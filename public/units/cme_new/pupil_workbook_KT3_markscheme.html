Enquiry Question (Page 2): How did US diplomacy, Sadat’s 1977 visit, and Camp David achieve peace with Egypt?
Page 3 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the Treaty of Washington (1979). [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the Treaty of Washington (signed on 26 March 1979 as the formal Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty) was Egypt’s immediate diplomatic suspension and boycott by the Arab world alongside lasting peace on Israel’s southern border. While Israel withdrew from the entire Sinai Peninsula in return for diplomatic recognition and US aid, Arab states accused Anwar Sadat of betraying Palestinian rights, suspended Egypt from the Arab League, and moved League headquarters from Cairo to Tunis. Domestic Islamist anger at the separate peace also led to Sadat’s assassination in Cairo on 6 October 1981.

Page 3 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Explain the importance of US President Carter and Camp David (1978) for peace between Israel and Egypt. [8 marks]
Edexcel Q3 Importance Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Generalised assertions • Level 2 (3–5m): Descriptive account with limited link to importance • Level 3 (6–8m): Two distinct, factually supported analytical paragraphs directly explaining why X mattered for Y.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, President Jimmy Carter’s personal mediation at Camp David in September 1978 rescued Egyptian-Israeli diplomacy when direct talks between Anwar Sadat and Menachem Begin had deadlocked after their 1977 Jerusalem and Ismailia visits. Sequestering both delegations for thirteen days at Camp David in Maryland, Carter shuttled tirelessly between their cabins to draft compromise wording when Begin and Sadat refused to speak face to face, backing his diplomacy with generous promises of annual US economic and military aid.

Second, the Camp David Accords signed on 17 September 1978 created the direct "Land for Peace" blueprint for the March 1979 Treaty of Washington — the first peace treaty between Israel and an Arab nation. By separating a concrete bilateral framework for complete Israeli withdrawal from the Sinai Peninsula and free navigation through Suez and Tiran from a vaguer five-year Palestinian autonomy plan, Camp David ended thirty years of war with Egypt and permanently removed the Arab world’s strongest military power from the coalition against Israel.

Enquiry Question (Page 4): How did Lebanon, the First Intifada, and Arafat’s 1988 UN speech break deadlock?
Page 5 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of Arafat’s renunciation of terrorism in a speech at the UN (1988). [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of Yasser Arafat’s December 1988 UN speech in Geneva renouncing terrorism was the immediate opening of official diplomatic dialogue between the United States and the PLO. By explicitly rejecting terrorism in all forms, accepting UN Resolutions 242 and 338, and confirming Israel’s right to exist in peace and security, Arafat satisfied Washington’s strict diplomatic conditions. President Reagan’s administration lifted its ban on official contact with the PLO, paving the way for Palestinian involvement in the 1990s peace process.

Page 5 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Explain the importance of the First Palestinian Intifada (1987–93) for attempts to find a solution in the Middle East. [8 marks]
Edexcel Q3 Importance Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Generalised assertions • Level 2 (3–5m): Descriptive account with limited link to importance • Level 3 (6–8m): Two distinct, factually supported analytical paragraphs directly explaining why X mattered for Y.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, the First Intifada shattered Israeli confidence that military occupation of Gaza and the West Bank could be maintained indefinitely without political compromise. Sparked in December 1987 after twenty years of military rule and coordinated by the underground UNLU through strikes, tax boycotts, and stone-throwing youths, the grassroots uprising exposed the limits of Defence Minister Yitzhak Rabin’s "Iron Fist" beatings and curfews. Global television images of heavily armed soldiers confronting unarmed teenagers damaged Israel’s international standing and convinced leaders like Rabin that a political settlement was necessary.

Second, the Intifada transformed Palestinian and regional diplomacy so two-state negotiations could begin. Pressured by local uprising leaders inside the occupied territories, the rise of rival Islamist group Hamas in Gaza, and Jordan dropping its claim to the West Bank in July 1988, Tunis-based Yasser Arafat proclaimed Palestinian independence alongside Israel in November 1988 and addressed the UN in Geneva in December 1988 to renounce terrorism and accept Resolution 242. This unlocked official US-PLO dialogue and laid the foundation for the Oslo Accords.

Key Topic 3.3: Madrid (1991), Oslo I (1993), PNA, Jordan Peace (1994) & Oslo II (1995)
Enquiry Question (Page 8): How far did the Oslo Accords (1993–95) and Jordan Peace Treaty achieve a solution?
Page 9 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the setting up of the Palestinian National Authority in 1994. [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of setting up the Palestinian National Authority (PNA) in 1994 under the Oslo I framework was the first Palestinian self-government over civil affairs and internal policing in the Gaza Strip and Jericho. Yasser Arafat returned from exile in Tunis to lead the PNA administration over schools, health, and local security as Israeli troops withdrew from those enclaves. However, core final-status issues — Jerusalem, permanent borders, refugees, and Israeli settlements — remained unresolved.

Page 9 • Question 2: Narrative Account [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Write a narrative account analysing attempts at a solution between Israel and the Palestinians in the years 1993–95. [8 marks]
Stimulus bullets printed: • The Oslo Accords (1993) • Oslo II (1995)
Edexcel Q2 Narrative Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Simple statements • Level 2 (3–5m): Chronological description with implicit links • Level 3 (6–8m): Organised beginning–middle–outcome sequence with explicit causal links & own knowledge beyond the two stimulus points.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

With public post-Madrid talks stalled in Washington, newly elected Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin backed secret negotiations with PLO representatives in Oslo, Norway in early 1993, agreeing mutual diplomatic recognition and a five-year interim peace framework. On 13 September 1993, President Bill Clinton hosted Rabin and Yasser Arafat on the White House lawn to sign the Oslo I Accord (Declaration of Principles), postponing Jerusalem, refugees, settlements, and final borders while launching phased self-rule.

Implementing Oslo I led directly to the setting up of the Palestinian National Authority (PNA) in 1994 as Arafat returned from Tunis to govern Gaza and Jericho, creating momentum that helped King Hussein and Rabin sign the October 1994 Israel-Jordan Peace Treaty. Building on this progress, Rabin and Arafat signed Oslo II in September 1995, expanding Palestinian self-rule across West Bank towns by dividing the territory into Area A (full Palestinian control), Area B (Palestinian civil and joint security control), and Area C (60% under full Israeli control, including settlements and roads).

However, leaving territory divided and core issues unresolved triggered deadly extremist violence on both sides aimed at wrecking the accords. After radical Jewish settler Baruch Goldstein murdered 29 Palestinian worshippers in Hebron in February 1994, Hamas and Islamic Jihad launched suicide bus bombings in Tel Aviv and Jerusalem, while right-wing Israeli fury at land concessions culminated on 4 November 1995 when Jewish extremist Yigal Amir assassinated Rabin after a Tel Aviv peace rally, dealing a severe blow to the peace process.

Page 10 • Question 1: Explain Two Consequences [8 Marks — 2 × 4m] Mark Each Consequence Independently out of 4
Explain two consequences of US involvement in the Gulf War (1991) for the Middle East peace process. [8 marks]
Pearson Edexcel Paper 2 Q1 Marking Rule: Mark Consequence 1 (0–4 marks) and Consequence 2 (0–4 marks) separately. Level 1 (1–2m): Simple/general statement • Level 2 (3–4m): Specific factual detail + explained causal outcome.
Consequence 1 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of US involvement in the 1990–91 Gulf War was the financial and diplomatic isolation of Yasser Arafat and the PLO, pushing them to compromise in peace talks. Because Arafat backed Iraqi leader Saddam Hussein against the US-led coalition, wealthy Gulf Arab states cut off financial subsidies to the PLO and expelled Palestinian workers. Bankrupt and weakened after also losing Soviet patronage, Arafat needed diplomatic breakthroughs at Madrid (1991) and Oslo (1993).

Consequence 2 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

A second consequence was unmatched American diplomatic authority across the Middle East that enabled Washington to convene the October 1991 Madrid Peace Conference. Having led a victorious international coalition and emerged from the Cold War as the sole superpower, President George H. W. Bush and Secretary of State James Baker brought Israeli, Syrian, Lebanese, and Jordanian-Palestinian delegations into direct face-to-face bilateral negotiations for the first time.

Page 11 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks] (Unscaffolded) 8 Marks (AO1: 4 + AO2: 4)
Explain the importance of changing superpower policies and the end of the Cold War for attempts to find a solution in the Middle East. [8 marks]
Level 3 (6–8 Marks): Two distinct, well-evidenced analytical paragraphs consistently explaining why the first factor mattered specifically for the second part of the question stem.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991 ended decades of Cold War superpower rivalry in which Moscow had armed and shielded Arab confrontation states and the PLO against compromise. Deprived of their superpower arms patron and Soviet diplomatic backing — while Arafat also lost Gulf Arab funding after supporting Saddam Hussein in the 1990–91 Gulf War — Syria and the PLO could no longer pursue a military option against Israel and had to enter American-led peace negotiations.

Second, the end of the Cold War left the United States as the sole global superpower able to sponsor face-to-face regional diplomacy without Soviet obstruction. Working alongside Moscow after the 1991 Gulf War victory, President George H. W. Bush and James Baker convened the October 1991 Madrid Peace Conference breaking historic taboos against direct talks, while President Bill Clinton’s administration later hosted and underwrote the September 1993 Oslo I Accord handshake and the October 1994 Israel-Jordan Peace Treaty.
