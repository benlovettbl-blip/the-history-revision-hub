Stage 1
Nov 1569–Jan 1570

Revolt of the Northern Earls

4,600 Rebels Held Mass; 450 Executed Afterwards

The Northern Earls marched 4,600 men into Durham Cathedral to hold Latin Mass and free Mary. Without Spanish aid, the revolt collapsed and Elizabeth ordered 450 rebels executed.

Papal Bull & Ridolfi Plot

1570 Bull Freed Catholics From Obeying Elizabeth

Pope Pius V’s 1570 bull Regnans in Excelsis excommunicated Elizabeth and urged rebellion. After spies exposed the 1571 Ridolfi Plot for a Spanish invasion, the Duke of Norfolk was executed.

Stage 3
1583–1584

Throckmorton & Bond of 1584

Spanish Envoy Mendoza Expelled From London

Francis Walsingham uncovered the 1583 Throckmorton Plot for the French Duke of Guise to invade with Spanish gold. Mendoza was expelled and nobles signed the 1584 Bond of Association.

Stage 4

Babington Beer-Barrel Trap

17 July 1586: Mary Backed Assassinating Elizabeth

In the 1586 Babington Plot, double agent Gifford passed coded letters hidden in beer barrels to Walsingham. Codebreaker Thomas Phelippes deciphered Mary’s letter backing Elizabeth’s murder.

8 Feb 1587: First Trial & Execution of a Queen

Convicted of treason at Fotheringhay Castle, Mary, Queen of Scots’ execution on 8 February 1587 in crimson martyrdom robes left her English claim to Philip II.

Catholic Figurehead Removed; Philip II Spurred On

Walsingham’s spies removed the Catholic rival inside England without Scottish King James VI declaring war. Yet executing an anointed Catholic monarch spurred Philip II to launch the Armada.

💡 The Big Story in One Sentence

After the 1570 Papal Bull turned plots toward foreign invasion and murder, Walsingham’s beer-barrel trap proved Mary Stuart’s treason and led to her 1587 execution.

1. Excommunication 1570 papal decree expelling Elizabeth and freeing Catholics from loyalty.
2. Bond of Association 1584 pledge to execute anyone linked to a plot on Elizabeth’s life.
3. Babington Plot 1586 Catholic plot whose decoded barrel letters proved Mary’s treason.
