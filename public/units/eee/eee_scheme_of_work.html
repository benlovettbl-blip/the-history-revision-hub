Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This lesson explores Elizabeth's attempt to navigate the treacherous religious divide in England by establishing a 'Middle Way'. The pedagogical focus is on helping students understand that the Religious Settlement of 1559 was a political compromise rather than a purely theological one, and to evaluate how the Church functioned as an instrument of state control before analysing the subsequent backlash from both extremes (Puritans and Catholics).

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain and evaluate Religious Divisions in 1558 (NOV 1558–JAN 1559): northern gentry, Marian exiles, Marian bishops.
  • Explain and evaluate The Act of Supremacy (MAY 1559): Act of Supremacy, Supreme Governor, Oath of Supremacy.
  • Explain and evaluate The Act of Uniformity (MAY 1559): Act of Uniformity, Book of Common Prayer, recusants.

Raleigh and the Colonisation of Virginia, 1584–1590

Central Enquiry: Why did Sir Walter Raleigh promote Virginia, and why did both the 1585 and 1587 Roanoke settlements fail?

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This final lesson of the Elizabethan unit examines Raleigh's Virginia colonies. It highlights the intersection of economic motives, disastrous planning (like the grounding of The Tiger), aggressive leadership, and the catastrophic impact of the Spanish Armada on supply lines, culminating in the mystery of the Lost Colony.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain and evaluate Raleigh’s Patent & Planning (1584): Sir Walter Raleigh, Virginia, Richard Hakluyt.
  • Explain and evaluate Recruiting Colonists & Leaders (APRIL 1585): 107 colonists, Sir Richard Grenville, Ralph Lane.
  • Explain and evaluate The First Roanoke Colony & Disaster (JUNE 1585–1586): Tiger, seed corn, Secotan fish traps.
