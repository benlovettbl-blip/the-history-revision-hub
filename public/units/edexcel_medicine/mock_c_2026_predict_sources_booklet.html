Sources for use with Section A.
Source A: From a clinical report by Captain Oswald Hope Robertson (US Army Medical Officer attached to the British Third Army RAMC), published in the British Medical Journal in June 1918, describing the first "blood depot" during the Battle of Cambrai in November 1917.
To prepare for the anticipated casualties before the Cambrai offensive, we established a forward blood depot at Casualty Clearing Station No. 48. Twenty-two units of universal Group O blood were collected in advance into sterilised glass bottles containing sodium citrate and dextrose (glucose) solution, packed in insulated wooden ammunition cases with ice and sawdust for up to twenty-six days. During the heavy fighting, we transfused twenty severely wounded British soldiers suffering from extreme haemorrhagic shock who had been judged too weak to survive surgery; eleven made a complete recovery.
