Enquiry 1: Mountains, Islands & Rival Poleis — Why Was Ancient Greece Divided Into City-States? (c. 800–500 BC)
Q1. Which famous gateway guarded the Bronze Age mainland fortress of Mycenae around 1250 BC? [ The Lion Gate / The Dragon Arch ]
Q2. Why did Ancient Greece develop into separate city-states (poleis) instead of one united kingdom?
Q3. Inside a Greek polis, what was the difference between the Acropolis and the Agora?
Q4. From which seafaring traders did Greeks adapt symbols around 800 BC to create their 24-letter alphabet?
Enquiry 2: Athens vs Sparta — Where Would You Rather Grow Up in Ancient Greece? (c. 508–430 BC)
Q5. What does the Ancient Greek word 'demokratia' (invented in Athens around 508 BC) literally mean? [ People-power (rule by the citizens) / Rule by two warrior kings ]
Q6. Which groups living in Athens were completely shut out from voting on the Pnyx hill?
Q7. What job did the slotted marble 'kleroterion' machine do in Athens?
Q8. At what age did Spartan citizen boys leave home to enter the tough 'agoge' military barracks?
Enquiry 3: Mount Olympus, Myths & The Ancient Olympics — How Did Religion Shape Greek Life? (776–400 BC)
Q9. Where did Ancient Greeks believe the twelve chief Olympian gods and goddesses lived? [ High on snow-capped Mount Olympus / At the bottom of the Nile River ]
Q10. Which gleaming marble temple was built atop Athens's Acropolis to honour wise warrior goddess Athena?
Q11. Why did Greek kings, generals, and travellers climb high up to the sanctuary of Delphi?
Q12. In what year were the first recorded Ancient Olympic Games held at Olympia in honour of Zeus?

Enquiry 4: Marathon, Thermopylae & Salamis — How Did Tiny City-States Defeat the Persian Empire? (490–479 BC)
Q13. What do historians call the tight shoulder-to-shoulder battle formation of Greek hoplites locking shields together? [ A phalanx / An ostrakon ]
Q14. Which legendary messenger ran roughly twenty-six miles from the battlefield of Marathon to Athens in 490 BC?
Q15. Which Spartan king led 300 Spartans and Greek allies to hold the narrow mountain pass of Thermopylae in 480 BC?
Q16. How many oarsmen rowed an ancient Greek wooden trireme, and what weapon sat at its waterline prow?
Enquiry 5: Theatre, Philosophy, Maths & Medicine — How Did Greek Thinkers Change Human Knowledge? (c. 460–322 BC)
Q17. Roughly how many spectators could sit inside the curved stone hillside amphitheatre at Epidaurus and still hear clearly? [ About 14,000 spectators / About 150 spectators ]
Q18. What were the two main types of plays performed by masked actors at Greek festivals for Dionysus?
Q19. What does the Greek word "philosopher" literally mean, and which three thinkers formed Athens’s famous chain of teachers?
Q20. On the island of Kos, how did physician Hippocrates change the way Greek doctors treated sick patients?
Enquiry 6: Alexander the Great & Our World Today — What Is Ancient Greece’s Lasting Legacy? (336–146 BC & Today)
Q21. Which famous philosopher tutored young Alexander of Macedon, and what was the name of Alexander’s beloved warhorse?
Q22. Which two famous landmarks stood in Egypt’s Hellenistic city of Alexandria?
Q23. What happened in 146 BC when the Roman Republic conquered mainland Greece?
Q24. How can you recognise a Greek Corinthian column capital compared with plain Doric and scrolled Ionic capitals?
