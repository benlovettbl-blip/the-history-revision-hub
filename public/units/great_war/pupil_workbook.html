Launched in February 1906 by the British Admiralty, HMS Dreadnought carried ten 12-inch guns and turbine engines, rendering all existing battleships instantly obsolete and resetting the global naval race to zero.
Significance: The naval arms race transformed Anglo-German trading partners into suspicious military rivals, driving Britain into the Triple Entente with France and Russia.

Question 3: Historiographical Analysis [8 marks • 10 mins]

HISTORIOGRAPHICAL ANALYSIS
Interpretation 1 • Professor Paul Kennedy • The Rise of Anglo-German Antagonism
“The naval race was the single most dangerous element in pre-1914 European diplomacy. For Britain, naval supremacy was not a luxury; it was the foundation of national survival and empire. When Germany began massing battleships directly across the North Sea, it transformed what had been friendly commercial relations into bitter existential suspicion. British leaders concluded that a German battlefleet could only be designed for one purpose: to destroy British naval power and dominate Europe.”
Interpretation 2 • Professor Christopher Clark • The Sleepwalkers
“Historians have greatly exaggerated the fatal impact of the naval race. By 1912, the battleship race was effectively over: Germany had abandoned the contest to concentrate resources on its land army, while Britain retained an insurmountable dreadnought lead. What ultimately triggered the catastrophe was not steel battleships in the North Sea, but unstable ethnic nationalism, crumbling imperial frontiers, and fatal alliance commitments in the volatile Balkan peninsula.”
Study Interpretations 1 and 2. How do they differ about the causes of the Anglo-German naval arms race? [4 marks]
PART B
Suggest one reason why Interpretations 1 and 2 differ about the causes of the naval arms race. [4 marks]
PART C
