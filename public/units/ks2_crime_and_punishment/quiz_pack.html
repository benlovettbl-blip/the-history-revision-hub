Enquiry 1: Roman Law, Twelve Tables and Bath Curse Tablets (AD 43–410)
Q1. In which year did Emperor Claudius’s Roman army invade Britain and bring written Roman law? [ AD 43 / 1066 ]
Q2. What was the name of Rome’s famous ancient code of written laws?
Q3. Inside which grand stone town building did a Roman judge hear serious court cases?
Q4. What was a Roman town judge called?
Enquiry 2: Anglo-Saxon Tithings, Wergild and Trial by Ordeal (AD 410–1066)
Q5. Around when did Roman rule in Britain end and the Anglo-Saxon era begin? [ Around AD 410 / Around AD 43 ]
Q6. At what age did an Anglo-Saxon boy have to join a local ten-person "tithing"?
Q7. How many neighbours belonged to one Anglo-Saxon "tithing"?
Q8. What had to happen immediately when someone blew a horn and shouted the "Hue and Cry"?
Enquiry 3: Norman Forest Laws, Sanctuary and Royal Courts (1066–1485)
Q9. Which two Anglo-Saxon peacekeeping rules did William the Conqueror KEEP after 1066? [ Tithings and the Hue and Cry / 1829 Bobbies and DNA tests ]
Q10. After 1066, where did Norman court fines go instead of being paid as wergild to victims’ families?
Q11. What new trial method did Normans introduce allowing wealthy nobles to settle disputes with swords or clubs?
Q12. What did William the Conqueror’s "Forest Laws" forbid ordinary villagers from doing in royal forests?
