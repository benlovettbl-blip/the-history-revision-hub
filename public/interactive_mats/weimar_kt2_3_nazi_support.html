Disciplinary Thinking Check (Paper 3 Synoptic Focus)

"Why was the Great Depression necessary, but not sufficient on its own, to explain the surge in Nazi popularity between 1929 and 1932?"

Grade 8/9 Examiner Model Answer

The Great Depression was the indispensable catalyst: it shattered the economic stability of the Golden Twenties, drove unemployment to 6.1 million, and exposed Weimar's institutional weakness as Brüning governed via Article 48 emergency decrees. However, economic misery alone does not explain why voters chose the Nazis rather than the Communists or other extremist parties. The decisive factors were the NSDAP's superior propaganda machinery under Goebbels, the cross-class nationalist appeal of Hitler's messianic leadership, and the middle-class terror of a Bolshevik revolution, which transformed economic grievance into political conversion.
