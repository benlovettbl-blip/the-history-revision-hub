The Succession Crisis & Broken Oath

Source A • Bayeux Tapestry

Edward the Confessor died childless on 5 January 1066. The Anglo-Saxon Witan council elected wealthy Earl Harold Godwinson, but Duke William claimed Harold had sworn a sacred oath to support him.

Narrative Block 3 14 Oct 1066

The Shield Wall & Feigned Retreat

Source D • Bayeux Tapestry

On Senlac Hill, Harold deployed a locked shield wall that repulsed cavalry charges for six hours. William ordered the famous feigned retreat, tricking the undisciplined fyrd into breaking formation.

Left Workbook Page • 3 Word Boxes
Box 1: Shield Wall [ Click Clue ]
Workbook Note: Impenetrable wall of overlapping Saxon shields atop Senlac Hill that held off Norman cavalry for hours.
Box 2: Feigned Retreat [ Click Clue ]
Workbook Note: Tactical trick where Norman cavalry faked panic, luring Saxon militia down the hill where they were surrounded.
Box 3: Combined Arms [ Click Clue ]
Workbook Note: William combined cavalry, infantry, and high-angle archers, whereas Harold relied solely on defensive infantry.
"While Harold's shield wall held strong, William's cunning feigned retreat broke their line and allowed his combined arms to annihilate the Saxon army."

Gold Clue • The Historian's Explanation ('So What?')

Explain why the feigned retreat was decisive: once the Saxon fyrd broke formation, their lack of horses meant they could neither defend themselves against cavalry nor catch up to retreat!

"A decisive factor in William’s victory was his mastery of combined arms. For over six hours on Senlac Hill, the Anglo-Saxon shield wall held firm against frontal cavalry charges. However, William exploited Saxon overconfidence by ordering a series of feigned retreats. When the undisciplined Saxon fyrd broke formation to pursue the 'fleeing' Normans, William’s heavy cavalry wheeled around and encircled them. Deprived of the defensive protection of their locked shields and exhausted from their 400-mile march from Stamford Bridge, the Saxon infantry was decimated, proving that William's tactical flexibility—not mere fortune—secured his triumph."
