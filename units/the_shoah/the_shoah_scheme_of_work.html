Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This foundational lesson establishes the humanity, individuality, and cultural richness of European Jewry before Nazi persecution. Grounded in international Holocaust historiography and age-appropriate KS3 enquiry principles, students must understand what was lost before exploring how it was destroyed. Do not begin with victimhood; begin with life, diversity, and belonging.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Describe the geographical and cultural diversity of the 9.5 million Jewish people living in pre-war Europe.
  • Explain the deep integration and patriotic contributions of German Jews prior to 1933.
  • Distinguish between historical religious anti-Judaism and modern pseudo-scientific racial antisemitism.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry explores how persecution escalated step-by-step between 1933 and 1939. Emphasise that genocide was not planned in 1933; the regime’s initial objective was forced emigration through legal ostracism and economic destruction. Guide students to examine how ordinary Germans responded to the isolation of their Jewish neighbours.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Analyze how the 1935 Nuremberg Laws stripped German Jews of citizenship and codified racial theory.
  • Explain the significance of the November Pogrom (Kristallnacht) as a turning point towards violent terror.
  • Evaluate the international failure of the 1938 Evian Conference and the desperate search for refuge.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry examines the transition from persecution to segregation following the outbreak of WWII. Focus on the concept of agency: despite horrific conditions of starvation and overcrowding, Jewish people organized soup kitchens, underground schools, orphanages (such as Janusz Korczak’s), and cultural events. Highlight the Oneg Shabbat archive as a profound act of resistance.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain how the invasion of Poland in 1939 transformed Nazi anti-Jewish policy into territorial segregation.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry addresses the critical turning point of the Holocaust: the summer of 1941. Discuss Christopher Browning’s findings in Ordinary Men (Reserve Police Battalion 101) to explore how ordinary middle-aged German policemen became mass murderers.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry addresses two central, interwoven themes: the industrialised mechanics of the "Final Solution" and the multi-faceted reality of Jewish resistance. Clarify terminology: distinguish strictly between concentration camps (Dachau, Sachsenhausen) and dedicated extermination/death camps (Treblinka, Belzec, Sobibor, Birkenau). Emphasise that resistance occurred under conditions of near-total starvation, isolation, and collective punishment.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain how the Wannsee Conference coordinated the bureaucratic and logistical machinery of the "Final Solution".
  • Analyze how dedicated extermination camps operated as industrial facilities of immediate murder.
  • Evaluate the heroism and significance of armed Jewish resistance in the Warsaw Ghetto and the death camps.
