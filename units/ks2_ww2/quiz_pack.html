TEACHER & PUPIL SELF-MARKING ANSWER KEY COMPLETE ANSWERS • QUESTIONS 1 TO 44
Enquiry 01: Outbreak 1939 & Home Front Mobilisation
  • Q1: Sunday 3 September 1939 (at 11:15am on BBC radio).
  • Q2: 1 September 1939 (at sunset, two days before war was declared).
  • Q3: 38 million gas masks (including red-and-blue "Mickey Mouse" masks for toddlers).
  • Q4: To stop glass shattering into dangerous flying splinters indoors during bomb blasts.
Enquiry 02: Operation Pied Piper & British Evacuation
  • Q5: Operation Pied Piper (launched on Friday 1 September 1939).
  • Q6: A brown paper/cardboard luggage label showing their name, school, and destination.
  • Q7: The Billeting Officer.
  • Q8: Any two from: Devon, Cornwall, Wales, Somerset, Dorset, East Anglia, or rural counties.
Enquiry 03: Pre-War Life & Rights Lost (1933–1938)
  • Q9: January 1933.
  • Q10: The Nuremberg Laws.
  • Q11: Synagogues were burned and Jewish-owned shop windows and homes were violently attacked.
  • Q12: All Jewish children were banned/expelled from attending German state schools.
Enquiry 04: Vera's Journey & The Kindertransport (1938–1939)
  • Q13: Nearly 10,000 children (between December 1938 and August 1939).
  • Q14: £50 per child (raised by charities and British foster sponsors).
  • Q15: Harwich port in Essex and London Liverpool Street Station.
  • Q16: Her English porcelain doll and her small autograph diary signed by her parents (plus family photo/clothes).
Enquiry 05: Who Were the Rescuers? (1938–1939)
  • Q17: 669 children (between March and August 1939).
  • Q18: Trevor Chadwick (working alongside Doreen Warriner in Prague and Babette Winton in London).
  • Q19: Nazi Germany invaded Poland that morning and all borders were immediately closed.
  • Q20: In the attic of his home.
Enquiry 06: The Blitz & Air Raids (1940–1941)
  • Q21: 7 September 1940 ("Black Saturday").
  • Q22: At least 15 inches of soil/earth.
  • Q23: The Morrison shelter.
  • Q24: Any two from: Coventry, Liverpool, Birmingham, Plymouth, Portsmouth, Clydebank (Glasgow), Hull, Bristol, Belfast.
Enquiry 07: Dig for Victory & Wartime Rationing (1940–1945)
  • Q25: Island Britain imported over two-thirds of its food by merchant ship, which U-boats attacked.
  • Q26: January 1940 (starting with bacon, butter, and sugar).
  • Q27: 1 fresh egg per week (supplemented by dried egg powder).
  • Q28: "Dig for Victory" (families grew unrationed potatoes, carrots, cabbages, and swedes for dishes like Woolton Pie).
Enquiry 08: Women at War (1941–1945)
  • Q29: December 1941.
  • Q30: Over 80,000 women.
  • Q31: Lumberjills.
  • Q32: Toxic TNT explosive powder turned their skin and hair bright yellow.
Enquiry 09: Secret Codebreakers & Bletchley Park (1941–1945)
  • Q33: Bletchley Park.
  • Q34: The Enigma machine.
  • Q35: The Bombe machine (inspired by earlier Polish mathematical work).
  • Q36: Around three-quarters (75%) were women; everyone signed the Official Secrets Act.
Enquiry 10: VE Day, Rebuilding Britain & Remembrance (1945)
  • Q37: Tuesday 8 May 1945.
  • Q38: Winston Churchill.
  • Q39: 15 August 1945 (Victory over Japan Day).
  • Q40: NHS opened in July 1948; food rationing ended in July 1954.
Enquiry 11: After the War — The Windermere Children (1945)
  • Q41: 10 converted RAF Short Stirling heavy bombers.
  • Q42: The Calgarth Estate in the Lake District.
  • Q43: Years of starvation made them fear food might run out overnight; gentle staff reassured them.
  • Q44: The '45 Aid Society; children's author Tom Palmer.
Classroom Marking & Stretch Guidance
  • 1. Credit Plain-English Phrasing: Award full marks where pupils express accurate historical meaning in their own words (e.g. "three-quarters" or "75%").
  • 2. Weekly Spaced Retrieval: Use 4 questions per week after each enquiry, or run Pages 1–3 as an end-of-unit team celebration.
  • 3. Green-Pen Self-Check: Encourage pupils to add missing dates or key terms in green pencil using Textbook pp.15–18.
  • 4. Oral Stretch Prompt: Ask pupils to connect two enquiries (e.g. how Bletchley Park helped Atlantic food convoys).
