WORLD WAR II: TEACHER GUIDE & SCHEME OF WORK

Complete Walk-In Planning Pack • 11 Chronological Enquiries (1933–1945) Fully Synced Across Textbook, Workbook, Worksheets & Web App

1. Statutory National Curriculum Purpose & Chronological Design

Designed for non-specialist and specialist UK primary teachers alike, this 11-lesson enquiry unit fulfils the Key Stage 2 statutory requirement for “a study of an aspect or theme in British history that extends pupils’ chronological knowledge beyond 1066”. Every resource follows a clear, child-friendly chronological arc: Britain’s outbreak preparations and evacuation in September 1939 (Lessons 1–2), stepping back to see pre-war Jewish family life and Kindertransport rescue in 1933–1939 (Lessons 3–5), surviving and contributing on the British Home Front in 1940–1945 (Lessons 6–9), and peace, remembrance, and post-war healing in May–August 1945 (Lessons 10–11).

2. Safeguarding & Holocaust Educational Trust (HET) Primary Principles

  • Humanise Before Persecution: Lesson 3 begins with joyful pre-war photographs (e.g. a 1935 boys’ football team) so pupils meet Jewish children as ordinary peers with hobbies and families before studying how Nazi laws removed rights step by step.
  • Age-Appropriate Focus on Rescue & Upstanders: Lessons 4, 5, and 11 follow nine-year-old Vera Schaufeld, Prague rescuers Nicholas Winton and Trevor Chadwick, and the post-war recovery of 300 teenagers by Lake Windermere—with zero graphic camp imagery or traumatic simulations.
  • Historical Accuracy Without Clichés: Avoids role-play distress games; contrasts British domestic evacuation (Operation Pied Piper) clearly with unaccompanied refugees fleeing persecution on the Kindertransport. Free teacher CPD at het.org.uk.

3. Complete Classroom Reprographics & Digital Sync Checklist (All 100% Aligned)

  • 20-Page Master Classroom Textbook (A4 / A3 Saddle-Stitch Booklet): Pages 4–14 provide one pure, distraction-free reading spread per lesson (320–360 words in large serif type), flanked by unit timelines, source gallery, maps, glossary, and 60 quick-fire checks.
  • Choose Either Print Format for Writing: Print the 28-Page Master Pupil Workbook once for the half-term (Missions 01–11 + 44-Q check + vocab + certificate) OR print individual 2-Page A4 Lesson Worksheets (L01–L11) trimmed along the dashed cutline for exercise books (with optional Sheet 3 suitcase crafts on L02 and L04).

WALK-IN 60-MINUTE LESSON PLANS LESSONS 01 & 02 OF 11
LESSON 01 (Sept 1939) Outbreak 1939 & Home Front Mobilisation
Textbook p.4 • Workbook pp.3–4 • Worksheet L01
Enquiry Question: Why did ordinary British families carry cardboard boxes and tape their windows in September 1939?
Key Vocabulary: Home Front • Blackout • Gas Mask • ARP Warden
Core Sources: Playground gas mask drill photo (IWM) & 1939 civilian rubber respirator + metal filter tin
Teacher Subject Knowledge: Blackout began at sunset on 1 Sept 1939 when Germany invaded Poland. On Sunday 3 Sept 1939 (11:15am), PM Neville Chamberlain announced war on BBC radio; minutes later a false-alarm siren sounded in London. 38 million gas masks were issued (including red/blue "Mickey Mouse" masks for toddlers), windows were taped against flying glass, and National Identity Cards were issued.
0–10m Do Now: 5-question prior-knowledge starter on 1939 Britain, maps of Europe & radio news.
10–25m Source Enquiry: Inspect playground drill photo & rubber gas mask. Hinge Q: Why issue masks before bombs fell?
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Mask Detective, Sept 1939 Timeline, Home Front Kit & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 paragraph answering the Enquiry Question + 5-question interactive quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils assume heavy bombing started on Day 1. Clarify the 8-month "Phoney War" before the 1940 Blitz.
Differentiation: Support: Sentence starters & labelled gas mask diagram. Stretch: Explain why morale mattered as much as physical protection.
LESSON 02 (Sept 1939) Operation Pied Piper & British Evacuation
Textbook p.5 • Workbook pp.5–6 • Worksheet L02 (+ Optional Suitcase Craft)
Enquiry Question: What was it like for 1.5 million British children to be sent away from home with a brown paper luggage label?
Key Vocabulary: Evacuation • Operation Pied Piper • Reception Area • Billeting Officer
Core Sources: Evacuees marching with luggage tags (Sept 1939) & UK Evacuation danger/reception map
Teacher Subject Knowledge: Launched on Friday 1 Sept 1939, Operation Pied Piper moved 1.5 million schoolchildren, teachers, and mothers with under-fives over three days from cities (London, Manchester, Liverpool, Glasgow) to rural reception areas (Devon, Cornwall, Wales). Billeting Officers matched children with hosts in village halls. Reminder: Rationing began in Jan 1940, so Sept 1939 evacuees did NOT pack ration books.
0–10m Do Now: Recap 1 Sept Blackout, 3 Sept broadcast, 38m gas masks & ARP wardens.
10–25m Map & Photo Enquiry: Trace routes west to Devon/Wales rather than Kent under bomber paths.
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Luggage Label Detective, Pied Piper Timeline, Sept 1939 Suitcase & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 Postcard Home to Mum and Dad + 5-question retrieval quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils think all evacuees were mistreated OR all had a holiday. Highlight genuinely mixed countryside experiences.
Differentiation: Support: Postcard writing frame & optional Sheet 3 Suitcase cut-out. Stretch: Contrast city vs rural host viewpoints.

LESSON 03 (1933–1938) Pre-War Life & Rights Lost (1933–1938)
Textbook p.6 • Workbook pp.7–8 • Worksheet L03
Enquiry Question: What was everyday life like for Jewish children in Europe before 1933, and how were their rights taken away step by step?
Key Vocabulary: Jewish Community • Citizenship • Nuremberg Laws • Kristallnacht
Core Sources: 1935 Jewish youth football team photo & c. 1935–37 German "Jews are not served here" exclusion sign
Teacher Subject Knowledge: HET-aligned foundation lesson: before Jan 1933, Jewish families across Germany, Austria, and Czechoslovakia lived ordinary, settled lives as proud citizens, neighbours, and classmates. Between 1933 and 1938—in peacetime—unfair Nazi laws removed rights step by step: bans from sports clubs/parks, loss of citizenship under the Sept 1935 Nuremberg Laws, Kristallnacht attacks (9–10 Nov 1938), and expulsion of Jewish pupils from state schools (15 Nov 1938).
0–10m Do Now: Recap Sept 1939 outbreak & evacuation, then step back on the timeline to 1933.
10–25m Photo Enquiry: Start with the 1935 boys’ football photo so pupils meet real children before studying persecution.
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Photo Detective, 1933–38 Timeline, Before vs Late 1938 & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 reflection on why studying pre-war life matters + 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils think Jewish families lived separately or lost rights only when war began in 1939. Show the 1933–38 peacetime steps.
Differentiation: Support: Clear dated timeline strip (1933, 1935, Nov 1938). Stretch: Connect Nov 1938 directly to why parents sought rescue.
LESSON 04 (1938–1939) Vera's Journey & The Kindertransport (1938–1939)
Textbook p.7 • Workbook pp.9–10 • Worksheet L04 (+ Optional Suitcase Craft)
Enquiry Question: How did 10,000 refugee children travel alone across Europe to safety in Britain in 1938–1939?
Key Vocabulary: Kindertransport • Refugee • Sponsor Guarantee • Foster Family
Core Sources: Kindertransport children arriving in Britain (Dec 1938) & European train/ferry route map
Teacher Subject Knowledge: Days after Kristallnacht (Nov 1938), Parliament waived visa delays for unaccompanied refugee children under 17 with a £50 private sponsor guarantee per child—but parents were not granted visas. Nearly 10,000 children travelled by train across the Dutch border, sailed from Hook of Holland to Harwich, and arrived at London Liverpool Street. Nine-year-old Vera Löwyová (later Vera Schaufeld) left Prague in May 1939 with her porcelain doll and autograph diary.
0–10m Do Now: Recap 1935 Nuremberg Laws, Kristallnacht & Nov 1938 school ban.
10–25m Route Enquiry: Follow Vera’s map from Prague across the Dutch border (hot cocoa from volunteers) to Harwich.
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Neck Tag Detective, Vera’s Timeline, One Small Suitcase & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 explaining why Kindertransport meant both safety and heartbreak + 5-Q quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils confuse British evacuation with Kindertransport. Contrast dates (1938–39 vs Sept 1939), language, and separation.
Differentiation: Support: Side-by-side Pied Piper vs Kindertransport comparison prompt. Stretch: Discuss the £50 guarantee barrier.

LESSON 05 (1938–1939) Who Were the Rescuers? (1938–1939)
Textbook p.8 • Workbook pp.11–12 • Worksheet L05
Enquiry Question: How did ordinary volunteers like Nicholas Winton and Trevor Chadwick rescue 669 children from Prague before war broke out?
Key Vocabulary: Upstander • Bystander • Visa Permit • Rescue Scrapbook
Core Sources: Nicholas Winton holding a child in Prague (1939) & Winton scrapbook 1939 foster-home newspaper appeal
Teacher Subject Knowledge: In Dec 1938, 29-year-old London clerk Nicholas Winton cancelled a skiing holiday to help refugees in Prague. Working evenings in London with his mother Babette while schoolteacher Trevor Chadwick and Doreen Warriner ran operations in Prague, the team brought 669 children on 8 trains (Mar–Aug 1939). War stopped the 9th train on 1 Sept 1939. Winton kept his scrapbook in his attic until 1988.
0–10m Do Now: Recap 10,000 Kindertransport children, £50 guarantee, Harwich & Vera Schaufeld.
10–25m Source Enquiry: Inspect Winton’s scrapbook appeal. Hinge Q: Why did Prague and London volunteers need each other?
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Scrapbook Detective, Rescue Chain, 50-Year Attic Secret & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 defining an "upstander" using historical evidence + 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils assume Winton acted alone or was a government minister. Emphasise ordinary teamwork (Chadwick, Warriner, foster parents).
Differentiation: Support: 3-link chain diagram (Prague -> Permits/£50 -> Foster Homes). Stretch: Reflect on why Winton stayed silent until 1988.
LESSON 06 (1940–1941) The Blitz & Air Raids (1940–1941)
Textbook p.9 • Workbook pp.13–14 • Worksheet L06
Enquiry Question: How did ordinary families protect themselves when bombs fell night after night during the Blitz?
Key Vocabulary: The Blitz • Anderson Shelter • Morrison Shelter • Incendiary Bomb
Core Sources: Garden Anderson shelter family photo, London Tube shelter photo & Blitz UK Target Map
Teacher Subject Knowledge: From 7 Sept 1940 ("Black Saturday") to May 1941, German aircraft bombed London for 57 consecutive nights alongside UK ports and industrial cities (Coventry, Liverpool, Birmingham, Plymouth, Clydebank, Hull, Bristol, Belfast). Families used corrugated steel Anderson shelters under 15 inches of garden soil, indoor steel Morrison table cages (1941), or deep Tube platforms while AFS firefighters tackled incendiary fires.
0–10m Do Now: Recap Blackout, evacuation reception areas & pre-war chronology.
10–25m Map & Shelter Enquiry: Locate bombed UK port/factory cities; compare Anderson, Morrison & Tube shelters.
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Shelter Detective, Dusk-to-Dawn Raid, 15-Inch Earth Design & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 paragraph on community survival during the Blitz + 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils think only London was bombed or everyone had a garden. Highlight Coventry/Liverpool/Clydebank and indoor Morrison cages.
Differentiation: Support: Annotated shelter comparison table. Stretch: Explain why ports like Liverpool were strategic targets.

LESSON 07 (1940–1945) Dig for Victory & Wartime Rationing (1940–1945)
Textbook p.10 • Workbook pp.15–16 • Worksheet L07
Enquiry Question: Why did the British government ration butter, sugar, and meat, and ask families to "Dig for Victory"?
Key Vocabulary: Rationing • Coupon Book • Merchant Convoy • Allotment
Core Sources: 1940 Ration Book coupons, Dig for Victory poster & Atlantic Convoy Lifeline Map
Teacher Subject Knowledge: Britain imported over two-thirds of its pre-war food by sea. As U-boats attacked Atlantic merchant convoys, the Ministry of Food introduced coupon rationing in Jan 1940 ("fair shares for all": adults received ~4oz bacon, 2oz butter, 2oz cheese, 8oz sugar, 2oz tea, and 1 fresh egg per week). Unrationed vegetables grown on 1.4 million "Dig for Victory" allotments made British children healthier than in the 1930s.
0–10m Do Now: Recap 7 Sept 1940 Blitz, 15 inches of soil, Morrison shelters & bombed UK cities.
10–25m Map & Artefact Enquiry: Connect garden potatoes to saving merchant ship space on Atlantic convoys.
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Ration Detective, Atlantic-to-Kitchen Timeline, Weekly Ration & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 explaining "fair shares for all" + 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils assume children starved or vegetables were rationed. Clarify that vegetables were unrationed and child health improved!
Differentiation: Support: Visual weekly ration box (1 fresh egg/week). Stretch: Link Bletchley Park & Atlantic convoys to home kitchens.
LESSON 08 (1941–1945) Women at War (1941–1945)
Textbook p.11 • Workbook pp.17–18 • Worksheet L08
Enquiry Question: How did women on farms, in forests, and in factories keep Britain fighting during the Second World War?
Key Vocabulary: Conscription • Land Girls • Lumberjills • Canary Girls
Core Sources: Wartime watercolour of Land Girls resting by a haystack & 1942 women munitions shell inspectors
Teacher Subject Knowledge: In Dec 1941, Britain passed the National Service Act calling up unmarried women aged 20–30. Over 80,000 Land Girls worked on farms in the Women's Land Army, Lumberjills in the Women's Timber Corps cut pit props for coal mines, nearly 1 million munitions workers ('Canary Girls' stained yellow by TNT) made shells and Spitfires, ATA women ferried warplanes, and hundreds of thousands served in the ATS, WAAF, and WRNS.
0–10m Do Now: Recap Atlantic U-boats, Jan 1940 rationing, 1 egg/week & Dig for Victory.
10–25m Visual Enquiry: Inspect the Land Army harvest watercolour & munitions factory photograph.
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Visual Detective, 1939–43 Call-Up Timeline, 4 Wartime Roles & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 explaining how women kept Britain fed and supplied + 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils think Land Girls did light summer gardening or all war work was optional. Highlight Dec 1941 call-up and heavy labour.
Differentiation: Support: Role-matching grid (Land Girls, Lumberjills, Canary Girls, ATS). Stretch: Discuss post-war impact on women’s roles.

LESSON 09 (1941–1945) Secret Codebreakers & Bletchley Park (1941–1945)
Textbook p.12 • Workbook pp.19–20 • Worksheet L09
Enquiry Question: How did secret codebreakers working in wooden huts at Bletchley Park help shorten the Second World War?
Key Vocabulary: Cipher • Enigma Machine • Bombe Machine • Official Secrets Act
Core Sources: German Enigma cipher machine & Alan Turing/Gordon Welchman Bombe machine run by Wrens
Teacher Subject Knowledge: At Bletchley Park ("Station X") in Buckinghamshire, 10,000 staff—around 75% of them women—worked in total secrecy. German Enigma machines had over 158 million million million settings and reset at midnight. Building on Polish mathematicians’ work, Alan Turing and Gordon Welchman designed the spinning-drum Bombe machine to find daily settings, steering Atlantic convoys away from U-boats. Staff kept silent for 30 years.
0–10m Do Now: Recap Dec 1941 National Service Act, Land Girls, Lumberjills & Canary Girls.
10–25m Machine Enquiry: Compare Enigma wheels with the Bombe. Hinge Q: Why would boasting once ruin everything?
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Machine Detective, Signal Pipeline, +1 Cipher & Secrecy, Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 linking Bletchley Park to saving lives at sea + 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils picture Turing working alone in a shed. Emphasise the 10,000-strong team (75% women) and Polish foundations.
Differentiation: Support: Step-by-step Y-Station -> Hut 8 -> Safe Convoy flow. Stretch: Explain why total secrecy lasted until the 1970s.
LESSON 10 (May–Aug 1945) VE Day, Rebuilding Britain & Remembrance (1945)
Textbook p.13 • Workbook pp.21–22 • Worksheet L10
Enquiry Question: Why was VE Day on 8 May 1945 a day of both joyful street parties and quiet sadness for British families?
Key Vocabulary: VE Day • VJ Day • Rationing • Remembrance
Core Sources: Crowds around a London bus on Whitehall (8 May 1945) & children at a neighbourhood VE Day street party
Teacher Subject Knowledge: Germany surrendered on 7 May 1945; Tuesday 8 May 1945 was VE Day. At 3:00pm Winston Churchill broadcast peace in Europe, streetlamps came back on, and neighbours pooled coupons for street parties amid bomb-damaged streets. Yet over 450,000 UK personnel and civilians had died, troops fought on in Asia until VJ Day (15 Aug 1945), the NHS opened in 1948, and rationing lasted until July 1954.
0–10m Do Now: Recap Bletchley Park, Enigma, Bombe drums & the Official Secrets Act.
10–25m Photo Enquiry: Contrast flags and smiling children with bombed buildings ("laughter through tears").
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (VE Day Detective, 1945–54 Timeline, Sharing & Remembrance, Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 balancing joy and bereavement on 8 May 1945 + 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils think all world fighting and rationing ended on 8 May 1945. Clarify VJ Day (15 Aug 1945) and rationing ending in 1954.
Differentiation: Support: Emotion sorting bank (relief, pride, grief, worry). Stretch: Include Commonwealth service and post-war rebuilding.

LESSON 11 (August 1945) After the War — The Windermere Children (1945)
Textbook p.14 • Workbook pp.23–24 • Worksheet L11
Enquiry Question: How did 300 child survivors begin to rebuild their lives by Lake Windermere in August 1945?
Key Vocabulary: Calgarth Estate • Sanctuary • Survivor Testimony • ’45 Aid Society
Core Sources: RAF Short Stirling airlift photo (Prague, Aug 1945), Rescue Flight Map & Tom Palmer's novel After the War
Teacher Subject Knowledge: In August 1945, Leonard Montefiore's charity arranged for 10 converted RAF Short Stirling heavy bombers to fly 300 orphaned Jewish child survivors from Prague to Carlisle, then by coach to the Calgarth Estate beside Lake Windermere. Dr Oscar Friedman, art therapist Marie Paneth, hot meals, English lessons, football, and donated bicycles helped them heal and form the lifelong '45 Aid Society.
0–10m Do Now: Bridge Kindertransport (1938–39) and VE Day (May 1945) to August 1945.
10–25m Archive & Book Enquiry: Explore Calgarth Estate & why staff patiently let children keep bread under pillows.
25–50m Application: Tasks 1–4 (Novel Detective, RAF Stirling Timeline, 1938 vs 1945 Comparison & Thinking Heads).
50–60m Plenary: Task 5 synthesis on kindness, resilience & rebuilding lives + final 5-question quiz.
Misconception Fix: Pupils confuse 1938–39 Kindertransport trains BEFORE war with Aug 1945 Windermere camp survivors flown AFTER VE Day.
Differentiation: Support: Clear 1938–39 vs Aug 1945 comparison box. Stretch: Explore how Tom Palmer used survivor testimony responsibly.
