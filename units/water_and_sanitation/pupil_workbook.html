Year 7 Enquiry • c.AD 43–Present

Water & Sanitation Through Time

Conquering Squalor, Cholera & The Battle for Public Health
Primary Visual Evidence • Epidemiological Cartography Dr John Snow (1854)

During the catastrophic 1854 Soho cholera epidemic, Dr John Snow mapped every recorded death as a black bar on a street plan, proving that water from the Broad Street pump—not airborne miasma—spread the lethal disease:

1. The Broad Street Pump Epicentre:
Clustered bars show over 500 fatal attacks occurred within a 250-yard radius of the Broad Street well pump.
2. The Broad Street Brewery Anomaly:
Snow found none of the brewery's 70 workers contracted cholera because they drank free malt liquor instead of pump water.
3. Leaking Cesspit Contamination:
Subsequent excavation revealed a defective Victorian brick cesspit leaking raw sewage just three feet from the public well.
4. Removal of the Pump Handle:
Snow persuaded parish guardians to remove the pump handle on 8 September 1854, halting the localized Soho outbreak.
Water Vector
Ingested pathogen replaces airborne miasma.
Spatial Data
Spot mapping establishes modern epidemiology.
Civic Reform
Scientific proof drives 1875 Public Health Act.
Disciplinary Significance: Snow’s spot map shattered the centuries-old miasma theory, demonstrating that public health breakthroughs require empirical data and state intervention rather than moral blame.
Disciplinary Hinge Question: Historical Evaluation
Did Dr John Snow's removal of the Broad Street pump handle prove the contagion was in the water, or did the miasma theory persist because Victorian authorities could not conceive of invisible living microbes before Pasteur?

Question 2: Explain Why [12 marks • 15 mins]

Extended Writing Practice
Explain why the Romans built sophisticated water supply and sanitation systems in Britain after AD 43. [12 marks]
Stimulus: The need for fresh water and hygiene in densely populated towns like Londinium Displaying imperial power, Romanitas, and luxury at sites like Fishbourne Palace (Support each factor with your own precise historical evidence)
1. URBAN DENSITY & HYGIENE
Explain how founding crowded stone towns (Londinium, York) forced Romans to construct gravity aqueducts and flushing latrines to prevent devastating epidemics.
2. IMPERIAL POWER & ROMANITAS
Explain how grand thermae, heated hypocausts, and luxury lead pipes at Fishbourne Palace were designed to display imperial supremacy (romanitas).
3. MILITARY READINESS & VERDICT
Weigh whether practical military survival or imperial vanity was more decisive in driving Roman engineering.
Key Terms: Aqueduct • Cloaca • Thermae • Miasma • Cesspit

Question 2: Explain Why [12 marks • 15 mins]

Extended Writing Practice
Explain why public health in Britain transformed dramatically between 1854 and 1900. [12 marks]
Stimulus: Dr John Snow's Soho investigation and spot map (1854) Louis Pasteur's Germ Theory and the 1875 Public Health Act (Support each factor with your own precise historical evidence)
1. SCIENTIFIC EPIDEMIOLOGY (SNOW)
Explain how Dr John Snow used spatial mapping around the Broad Street pump to prove cholera was an ingested waterborne poison.
2. BACTERIOLOGY (PASTEUR & KOCH)
Explain how Pasteur's Germ Theory (1861) and Koch's isolation of Vibrio cholerae (1884) permanently destroyed miasma dogma.
3. COMPULSORY STATE REFORM (1875)
Explain how the compulsory 1875 Public Health Act ended laissez-faire and forced all councils to provide clean water and sewers.
