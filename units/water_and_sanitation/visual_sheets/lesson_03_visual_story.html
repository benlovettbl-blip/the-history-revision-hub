Tudor London’s Huge Boom

1st Flush Water Closet Built for Elizabeth I

Myddelton’s Paid New River

38-Mile Canal Piped Water to Rich Homes

In 1613, Sir Hugh Myddelton opened the 38-mile New River canal into Islington. Hollow elm-trunk pipes supplied clean water, but only to rich paying homes.

Pepys’s Leaking Cellar Cesspit

Cracked Brick Vaults Poisoned Parish Wells

In 1660, diarist Samuel Pepys stepped into raw sewage leaking through his cellar wall! Cracked brick cesspits poisoned wells while nightsoil carts cleared waste.

1665 Plague & Miasma Mistakes

240,000 Cats & Dogs Killed While Rats Spread

During the 1665 Great Plague, officials still blamed miasma and killed 240,000 cats and dogs! Killing rat-hunters helped rat fleas kill over 100,000 Londoners.

By 1666

Inventions Needed Public Sewers

Over 100,000 Plague Deaths Tallied in London

Weekly Bills of Mortality proved overcrowded Stuart London had grown deadlier. Clever inventions could not save lives without public sewers or germ science.

💡 The Big Story in One Sentence

Thinkers invented the 1596 flush toilet and 1613 New River canal, yet without public sewers or germ science, leaking cesspits and the 1665 Plague ravaged London.
