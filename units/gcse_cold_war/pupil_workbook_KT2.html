Cover Source Study October 1961
Tank Standoff Across Friedrichstrasse at Checkpoint Charlie, Berlin
Two months after East German troops sealed the border on 13 August 1961, US and Soviet tanks stood engines running just 100 metres apart at Checkpoint Charlie—symbolising the razor-thin margin between stalemate and nuclear war.
3 Details to Spot in This Photograph:
1. Soviet battle tanks positioned directly behind stacked concrete barrier beams blocking Friedrichstrasse.
2. Berlin U-Bahn ("U") station sign and clock overhead showing an everyday city sliced in two.
3. Tank commanders in padded helmets peering from open turret hatches during the tense 16-hour standoff.
Class Discussion: Why did building the Berlin Wall simultaneously ease the risk of war over Berlin while handing the West a propaganda victory?

Two separate 4-mark consequences. State each consequence, give a specific date/fact, and explain what it led to (10 mins).

3 chronological paragraphs (Beginning → Key Developments → Outcome) linked by causal phrases + own knowledge (12 mins).

Answer 2 of 3 choices. For each 8-mark choice, write two paragraphs explaining why X was important for Y (25 mins).

Events of the Cuban Missile Crisis

Impact of the construction of the Berlin Wall on US-Soviet relations; Kennedy’s visit to West Berlin in 1963
Consequences of the Cuban Missile Crisis (incl. the ‘hotline’); Attempts at arms control: the Limited Test Ban Treaty (1963); the Outer Space Treaty (1967); & the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968)
International reaction to Soviet measures in Czechoslovakia
