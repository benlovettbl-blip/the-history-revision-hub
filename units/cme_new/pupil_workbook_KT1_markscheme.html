Key Topic 1.1: Conflicting Demands, Insurgency & UN Resolution 181 (1945–47)
Enquiry Question (Page 2): Why did Britain hand the Palestine Mandate over to the United Nations in 1947?
Page 3 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the bombing of the King David Hotel (1946). [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the bombing of the King David Hotel on 22 July 1946 was a sharp collapse in British public and political support for keeping the Mandate. Because the Irgun explosion demolished the six-floor British military and civil headquarters in Jerusalem and killed 91 people, British MPs and newspapers questioned why 100,000 troops were policing an ungovernable conflict during post-war economic exhaustion. Shock at the civilian toll also led the Haganah to end its formal alliance with the Irgun while pushing Britain closer to handing Palestine to the UN in February 1947.

Page 3 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Explain the importance of the conflicting interests and demands of Jews and Arabs within the British Mandate for Britain’s decision to withdraw from Palestine in 1947. [8 marks]
Edexcel Q3 Importance Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Generalised assertions • Level 2 (3–5m): Descriptive account with limited link to importance • Level 3 (6–8m): Two distinct, factually supported analytical paragraphs directly explaining why X mattered for Y.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, irreconcilable Jewish and Arab demands over immigration and statehood made any peaceful British political settlement impossible after 1945. With 250,000 Holocaust survivors stranded in European DP camps, the Jewish Agency and US President Harry S. Truman urged Britain to admit 100,000 refugees immediately and establish a Jewish state, whereas the Arab Higher Committee demanded an immediate halt to Jewish immigration and Arab majority independence. Caught between American pressure and Foreign Secretary Ernest Bevin’s fear of an Arab uprising threatening British Middle Eastern oil supplies, Britain enforced the 1939 White Paper cap of 1,500 migrants a month and alienated both sides.

Second, these conflicting nationalist goals escalated into armed Zionist insurgency that exhausted war-weary Britain militarily and morally. Outraged by immigration limits, the Haganah, Irgun, and Lehi sabotaged railways and bridges, bombed the King David Hotel in July 1946 (killing 91 people), and hanged two kidnapped British sergeants in July 1947, while the Royal Navy’s forced return of 4,500 SS Exodus survivors to Germany caused worldwide outrage. Stationing 100,000 soldiers in Palestine while financially exhausted after World War Two proved unsustainable, forcing Britain to hand the Mandate to the United Nations in 1947.

Key Topic 1.2: Statehood, 1948–49 War, Al-Nakba, Rhodes Armistices & Consolidation
Enquiry Question (Page 4): How did Israel win the 1948–49 war, and how did its aftermath reshape the region?
Page 5 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the refugee status of Palestinian Arabs following the 1948–49 war. [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the refugee status of over 700,000 Palestinian Arabs after the 1948–49 war was long-term displacement in overcrowded camps across neighbouring Arab territories. Because over 400 villages were depopulated during Plan Dalet and Israel permanently barred repatriation to preserve a Jewish majority, the UN established UNRWA to provide emergency relief in Gaza, the West Bank, Jordan, Syria, and Lebanon. Statelessness in these border camps directly fuelled cross-border fedayeen guerrilla raids and Israeli military reprisals during the early 1950s.

Page 5 • Question 2: Narrative Account [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Write a narrative account analysing the key events of the Arab-Israeli war in the years 1948–49. [8 marks]
Stimulus bullets printed: • The creation of Israel (May 1948) • Arab invasion and armistice agreements
Edexcel Q2 Narrative Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Simple statements • Level 2 (3–5m): Chronological description with implicit links • Level 3 (6–8m): Organised beginning–middle–outcome sequence with explicit causal links & own knowledge beyond the two stimulus points.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

On 14 May 1948, hours before the British Mandate expired, David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the State of Israel in Tel Aviv and won immediate recognition from the USA and USSR. This directly triggered a multi-front invasion at dawn on 15 May by five Arab League armies — Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon, and Iraq — forcing outnumbered Israeli defenders lacking heavy artillery and aircraft into a desperate battle for survival.

However, the divided Arab armies lacked a single command, allowing Israel to hold out until UN mediator Count Folke Bernadotte brokered the four-week First Truce in June 1948. Israel used this pause decisively: Ben-Gurion merged Zionist militias into a unified Israel Defense Forces (IDF) and imported thousands of rifles, machine guns, and fighter aircraft through a Soviet-approved Czech arms deal, enabling reinforced Israeli divisions to break Arab lines when fighting resumed in July.

As Israeli offensives expanded control and over 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled in the Nakba, UN mediator Ralph Bunche brokered separate bilateral armistice agreements on Rhodes between February and July 1949. These Rhodes Armistices drew the Green Line leaving Israel with 78% of Mandatory Palestine while Jordan annexed the West Bank and East Jerusalem and Egypt occupied Gaza — ending open combat without formal peace treaties as Egypt continued barring Israeli ships from the Suez Canal.

Key Topic 1.3: Gaza Raid (1955), Czech Arms, Suez Crisis (1956) & The UAR (1958)
Enquiry Question (Page 8): How did the 1956 Suez Crisis weaken European empires and make Nasser leader of the Arab world?
Page 9 • Question 1 [4 Marks] Level 1: 1–2m | Level 2: 3–4m (AO1 + AO2)
Explain one consequence of the Israeli attack on Gaza in February 1955. [4 marks]
Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the Israeli attack on Gaza on 28 February 1955 (Operation Black Arrow) was Egypt’s pivot to Soviet weaponry via the Czech arms deal. Because Israeli paratroopers killed 38 Egyptian soldiers and humiliated President Gamal Abdel Nasser while Western powers tied weapons sales to Cold War alliances, Nasser signed the September 1955 Czech arms deal for 200 Soviet MiG-15 jets and 300 tanks. This ended the Western arms monopoly in the Middle East and escalated border tension as Nasser tightened the blockade of the Straits of Tiran.

Page 9 • Question 2: Narrative Account [8 marks • 12 mins] 8 Marks
Write a narrative account analysing the key events of the Suez Crisis (1956). [8 marks]
Stimulus bullets printed: • Nasser’s nationalisation of the Suez Canal (July 1956) • Israeli attack on Sinai (October 1956)
Edexcel Q2 Narrative Tariff (8 Marks — AO1: 4, AO2: 4): Level 1 (1–2m): Simple statements • Level 2 (3–5m): Chronological description with implicit links • Level 3 (6–8m): Organised beginning–middle–outcome sequence with explicit causal links & own knowledge beyond the two stimulus points.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

After Nasser bought Soviet weapons in the 1955 Czech arms deal and recognised Communist China in May 1956, US Secretary of State John Foster Dulles cancelled promised World Bank loans for Egypt’s Aswan High Dam in July 1956. In retaliation on 26 July 1956, Nasser nationalised the Anglo-French Suez Canal Company to fund the dam from shipping tolls, alarming Britain and France over their strategic oil waterway while Israel sought to stop fedayeen raids and reopen the blockaded Straits of Tiran.

This shared hostility led British Prime Minister Anthony Eden, French leaders, and David Ben-Gurion to sign the secret Sèvres Agreement outside Paris in late October 1956. Under this pre-arranged plot, Israeli paratroopers invaded Sinai on 29 October toward the canal, giving Britain and France a staged pretext to issue an ultimatum and land paratroopers at Port Said after Nasser refused to pull back.

However, US President Dwight D. Eisenhower was furious at not being consulted during the Hungary crisis and threatened to block IMF loans and collapse the British pound alongside Soviet warnings. Financial pressure forced Britain and France into a humiliating ceasefire on 6 November 1956 and Israeli withdrawal in early 1957; UNEF peacekeepers reopened the Straits of Tiran, while Nasser kept the canal as hero of Pan-Arabism and united Egypt with Syria in the United Arab Republic in 1958.

Key Topic 1 Cold Mini-Mock (1945–63)
Page 10 • Question 1: Explain Two Consequences [8 Marks — 2 × 4m] Mark Each Consequence Independently out of 4
Explain two consequences of Israel passing the Law of Return (1950). [8 marks]
Pearson Edexcel Paper 2 Q1 Marking Rule: Mark Consequence 1 (0–4 marks) and Consequence 2 (0–4 marks) separately. Level 1 (1–2m): Simple/general statement • Level 2 (3–4m): Specific factual detail + explained causal outcome.
Consequence 1 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

One consequence of the Law of Return (July 1950) was a rapid demographic surge that more than doubled Israel’s Jewish population within four years. By granting every Jewish person worldwide the legal right to settle in Israel with immediate citizenship and organising airlifts such as Operation Magic Carpet from Yemen and Iraq, Israel’s population grew from 650,000 to over 1.4 million by 1952. This secured Israel’s Jewish majority and expanded manpower for frontier settlements and the reserve IDF.

Consequence 2 — Grade 9 Model Answer (4 / 4 Marks):

A second consequence was severe economic strain and housing shortages that made Israel heavily reliant on American financial aid. Absorbing hundreds of thousands of penniless Holocaust survivors and diaspora refugees forced the Israeli government to impose food rationing and house newcomers in crowded tented transit camps (ma’abarot). To prevent economic collapse while defending hostile borders, Israel depended on a $100 million US Export-Import Bank loan and private fundraising from American Jewish communities.

Page 11 • Question 3: Explain the Importance [8 marks] (Unscaffolded) 8 Marks (AO1: 4 + AO2: 4)
Explain the importance of Nasser for Egypt’s leadership of the Arab world in the years 1955–63. [8 marks]
Level 3 (6–8 Marks): Two distinct, well-evidenced analytical paragraphs consistently explaining why the first factor mattered specifically for the second part of the question stem.
Grade 9 Model Answer (8 / 8 Marks):

First, Gamal Abdel Nasser made Egypt the champion of Arab anti-colonial defiance by standing up to Western powers between 1955 and 1956. Refusing Western alliance conditions after Israel’s 1955 Gaza raid, Nasser broke the Western arms monopoly through the September 1955 Soviet-backed Czech arms deal and nationalised the Anglo-French Suez Canal Company on 26 July 1956 after the USA cancelled Aswan High Dam loans. When US and Soviet pressure forced British, French, and Israeli forces to withdraw while Egypt kept the canal, Nasser emerged from the Suez Crisis with immense prestige across the Middle East.

Second, Nasser turned that prestige into concrete Pan-Arab political leadership, shown most clearly by the creation of the United Arab Republic (UAR) in February 1958. Inspired by Nasser’s triumph over European imperialism, Syria merged with Egypt into a single Arab state under Nasser’s presidency, proving Cairo’s unmatched influence over Arab nationalism and opposition to Israel. Even after Syria left the union in 1961, Egypt remained the diplomatic leader of the Arab states heading into the 1964 Cairo Summit.
