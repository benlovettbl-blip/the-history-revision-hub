E01

German Unification, Franco-Prussian War & Bismarck (1871–1890)

Central Enquiry: How did German unification in 1871 unsettle the balance of power in Europe?
Curriculum / Spec Anchor: KS3 National Curriculum (Challenges 1901–Present): Pre-1914 European geopolitics — German unification (1871), Alsace-Lorraine, French revanche, and Bismarckian…
Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry introduces students to the foundational geopolitical shockwave of modern European history: the creation of a unified, militaristic German Empire in 1871. Students examine how Otto von Bismarck used three calculated wars to unite 39 sovereign states under Prussian dominance, culminated in the coronation of Kaiser Wilhelm I inside the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, and provoked permanent French hostility through the annexation of Alsace-Lorraine.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Understand how Otto von Bismarck used 'blood and iron' and the Franco-Prussian War to unify the German states.
  • Analyze why the annexation of Alsace-Lorraine created permanent French 'Revanche' and shattered the European balance of power.
  • Evaluate how Bismarck's post-1871 alliance system sought to prevent a two-front encirclement.

Imperial Rivalry, the Scramble for Africa & the Moroccan Crises (1884–1911)

Central Enquiry: How did colonial rivalry in Africa and Kaiser Wilhelm’s ambitions fuel tension?
Curriculum / Spec Anchor: KS3 National Curriculum (Challenges 1901–Present): Imperialism, the 1884–85 Berlin Conference, Kaiser Wilhelm II's Weltpolitik, and the 1905 and 1911 Moroccan…
Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry explores how imperial competition outside Europe poisoned relations between the Great Powers. In 1884, Bismarck convened the Berlin Conference to peacefully divide Africa under the doctrine of "effective occupation." But when Kaiser Wilhelm II dismissed Bismarck in 1890 and launched "Weltpolitik" demanding Germany’s "place in the sun," imperial expansion became aggressive and confrontational.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain why European powers partitioned Africa at the 1884–85 Berlin Conference.
  • Analyze how Kaiser Wilhelm II’s policy of "Weltpolitik" altered German foreign policy.
  • Evaluate how the Moroccan Crises cemented the Anglo-French Entente and isolated Germany.

The Anglo-German Naval Arms Race & HMS Dreadnought (1898–1914)

Central Enquiry: Why did the Dreadnought battleship race poison relations between Britain and Germany?
Curriculum / Spec Anchor: KS3 National Curriculum (Challenges 1901–Present): Militarism, the 1889 Two-Power Standard, Tirpitz's Risk Theory, and the 1906 HMS Dreadnought arms race…
Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry investigates the technological and strategic arms race that turned Great Britain and Germany from natural commercial partners into bitter existential rivals. For island Britain, naval supremacy was vital to safeguard imported food supplies and imperial sea lanes. When Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz persuaded the Kaiser to build the High Seas Fleet using his "Risk Theory," Britain viewed it as an intolerable threat to the North Sea.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain why naval supremacy was vital for Great Britain’s national survival.
  • Analyze how Admiral Tirpitz’s "Risk Theory" aimed to challenge the Royal Navy.
  • Evaluate the paradoxical impact of the launch of HMS Dreadnought in 1906.

Curriculum / Spec Anchor: KS3 National Curriculum (Challenges 1901–Present): Triple Alliance vs Triple Entente, Ottoman decline, Pan-Slavism, the 1908 Bosnian Crisis, and the 1912–13 Balkan…
Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This enquiry examines how Europe was divided into two armed, rival treaty coalitions: the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and the Triple Entente (Britain, France, Russia). While originally intended as defensive deterrents to maintain peace through mutual balance, these rigid military pacts created an explosive tripwire mechanism.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain how the Triple Alliance and Triple Entente divided Europe into two armed camps.
  • Analyze why the Balkans became the "Powder Keg of Europe".
  • Evaluate the consequences of the 1908 Bosnian Crisis and the 1912–13 Balkan Wars.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This final enquiry investigates the dramatic thirty-seven-day countdown from the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo to the general outbreak of the First World War in August 1914. Students analyze the mechanics of short-term escalation: the Black Hand assassination, Kaiser Wilhelm II’s unconditional "Blank Cheque" to Austria, the deliberately intolerable Ten-Point Ultimatum delivered to Serbia, the inexorable pull of railway mobilization timetables, and Germany’s execution of the Schlieffen Plan violating Belgian neutrality.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Explain why Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914.
  • Analyze how Germany’s "Blank Cheque" escalated a regional conflict into a continental crisis.
  • Evaluate how rigid railway mobilization timetables and the Schlieffen Plan forced Great Britain into the war.

Subject Knowledge Primer (Non-Specialist Walk-In Briefing)

This masterclass equips Year 9 students for their 30-minute formal written assessment: "Why did war break out in 1914?". Students select three developed reasons from four core curriculum strands: (1) German Unification and Bismarck; (2) Imperial Rivalry and the Scramble for Africa; (3) The Anglo-German Naval Arms Race and HMS Dreadnought; and (4) The Alliance System and the Sarajevo Assassination. Emphasize to lower-ability pupils that strictly 3 developed PEEL paragraphs are required with NO conclusion.

Core Learning Objectives
  • Select three developed historical reasons from the four assessment options and construct a compliant 1-page A4 revision notes sheet.
  • Apply the PEEL technique (Point, Evidence, Explain, Link) with differentiated sentence frames to explain causation.
  • Structure 3 developed paragraphs within the 30-minute time constraint without wasting time on a conclusion.
