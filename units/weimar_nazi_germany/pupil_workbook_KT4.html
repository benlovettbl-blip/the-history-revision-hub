Question 3(a): Source Utility [8 marks • 12 mins]

SOURCE B
Front-page article in the Nazi newspaper Völkischer Beobachter, 3 May 1933
From the official Nazi Party newspaper, published the day after SA stormtroopers raided and dissolved all independent German trade unions, May 1933.
“German workers! Your trade unions were corrupted by Marxist class hatred. Today, the National Socialist state has liberated you! By order of the Führer, the German Labour Front (DAF) under Dr. Robert Ley has assumed leadership of all working Germans. Henceforth, employers and workers are united as comrades in the service of the nation. Strike and lockout are abolished forever!”
SOURCE C
Contemporary photograph of Reich Labour Service (RAD) workers building an Autobahn, 1936
From a photograph published in a German magazine, 1936. It shows young men of the Reich Labour Service (RAD) clearing ground for the construction of a new autobahn.
Under the Reich Labour Service Law of June 1935, all young men aged 18 to 25 were compelled to serve six months in the RAD before entering the military. They lived in military-style barracks, wore uniforms, and performed hard manual labour—draining wetlands, planting forests, and building motorways—for pocket money rather than a regular wage.

HISTORICAL INTERPRETATIONS 1 AND 2 Target: How and why historians differ (AO4)
INTERPRETATION 1
From an account by historian Richard Grunberger, A Social History of the Third Reich (1971)
“For the vast majority of German industrial workers, the Nazi era brought tangible improvements in everyday life. Unemployment was eliminated within four years, replacing the terror of the Great Depression with guaranteed employment and steady earnings. Through the ‘Strength Through Joy’ (KdF) organization, millions of ordinary workers enjoyed subsidised theatre tickets, sports facilities, and cruise holidays to Norway and Italy that had previously been the exclusive preserve of the rich. The ‘Beauty of Labour’ campaign renovated factory canteens, washrooms, and ventilation, creating genuinely improved working conditions.”
INTERPRETATION 2
From an account by historian Tim Mason, Social Policy in the Third Reich (1993)
“The claim that German workers experienced a higher standard of living under National Socialism is thoroughly misleading. In return for full employment, workers surrendered all basic rights and freedoms. Real wages remained below 1928 levels while compulsory deductions for the Labour Front, winter relief, and state insurance consumed up to thirty percent of pay packets. Furthermore, the average working week rose from forty-three hours in 1933 to forty-seven hours in 1939. Stripped of the right to strike and tied to their jobs by the Labour Book, workers suffered declining living standards.”
Study Interpretations 1 and 2. They give different views about the standard of living of German workers in the years 1933–1939. What is the main difference between these views? [4 marks] Score: [      / 4 ]
