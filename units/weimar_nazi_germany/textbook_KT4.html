Commanded the Hitler Youth (HJ) for boys and League of German Maidens (BDM), reaching over 7–8 million members by 1939.

Bernhard Rust (1883–1945) Education Minister

Purged non-compliant teachers via the National Socialist Teachers’ League (97% membership) and founded elite Napolas schools.

Hjalmar Schacht & Hermann Göring Economic Architects

Schacht’s New Plan & Mefo bills financed early recovery; Göring’s 1936 Four-Year Plan prioritised autarky and rearmament.

Robert Ley (1890–1945) Head of the DAF

Controlled wages and workplace discipline through the German Labour Front, Beauty of Labour (SdA), and Strength Through Joy (KdF).

Julius Streicher (1885–1946) Editor of Der Stürmer

Published virulent antisemitic caricatures and children’s books (The Poisonous Mushroom) conditioning racial hatred.

Kinder, Küche, Kirche & Mother’s Cross "Children, Kitchen, Church"; Bronze (4), Silver (6), and Gold (8 children) medals awarded each 12 August.
HJ & BDM Hitler Youth (military drill, camping, rifle practice) and League of German Maidens (domestic skills, fitness for motherhood).
RAD (Reich Labour Service) Compulsory 6-month uniformed manual labour for men aged 18–25 on drainage, farming, and autobahns for pocket money.

Kristallnacht (9–10 Nov 1938) Turning point from legal discrimination to open, state-orchestrated physical violence and mass concentration camp internment.

KEY ENQUIRY “How did the Nazis attempt to reverse the freedoms of Weimar women, and why did their policies ultimately contradict their own economic goals?”
STAGE 1 The Domestic Ideal: Kinder, Küche, Kirche and the Anti-Feminist Reaction 1933
“How did the regime attempt to remove women from public professions?”

Reversing Weimar female emancipation, the Nazi regime banned women from legal and medical careers, capped female university entry at 10%, and promoted Kinder, Küche, Kirche (Children, Kitchen, Church). Hitler rejected the Weimar 'New Woman', condemning female career independence as a threat to German birth rates. Steering women back into traditional domestic roles and motherhood, Reich Women's Leader Gertrud Scholtz-Klink merged all female societies into the Nazi Women's League. Six million members attended courses in home cooking, thrifty housekeeping, infant care, and racial duty.

STAGE 2 The Law for the Encouragement of Marriage: Subsidies and Childbirth Loans 1933
“How were financial loans used to encourage childbearing and lower unemployment?”

Confronted by falling birth rates, the government enacted the Law for the Encouragement of Marriage in June 1933 to subsidise large Aryan families. Young newlywed Aryan couples received interest-free 1,000-mark marriage loan coupons — worth nine months' factory wages — for furniture, provided the bride left her job. To boost family size directly, the law granted 25% loan forgiveness per child born, so having four children cancelled the entire debt. Mothers of eight or more children later won gold honours, while childless couples faced extra income tax surcharges.

Nazi Policies Towards Women — Escalation & Consequences

Stages 3–5
STAGE 3 The Mother's Cross and the Lebensborn Racial Breeding Program 1935–1938
“How did honors and breeding centres aim to expand the Aryan population?”

To glorify large families, Hitler introduced the Cross of Honour of the German Mother (Mutterkreuz) in 1938, awarded each year on his mother's birthday. Medals gave four for bronze and six for silver, plus a revered gold cross for 8 or more children, earning seats of honour and Hitler Youth salutes. Beyond marriage, Heinrich Himmler's SS founded the secretive Lebensborn maternity programme in 1935. Starting as private maternity homes for unmarried Nordic women, Lebensborn encouraged unmarried Aryan women to have "genetically pure" children with SS officers in state clinics.

STAGE 4 Forced Sterilisation and Eugenic Hygiene: The Hereditary Health Law 1933
“How did eugenics legislation enforce biological selection on German families?”

Alongside gold medals requiring Hitler Youth boys to salute her and later Duty Year rules (a policy forcing young women to work on farms or in factories), eugenics barred "unfit" births. Under the July 1933 Hereditary Health Act, Genetic Health Courts ordered compulsory sterilisation of individuals diagnosed with hereditary illnesses or disabilities. Under this harsh law, over 400,000 German citizens — mostly working-class people — were sterilised against their will. Courts targeted hereditary blindness, deafness, epilepsy, schizophrenia, and severe alcoholism, while the 1935 Marital Health Law required genetic fitness certificates before marriage.

STAGE 5 The Policy Reversal: Four-Year Plan Rearmament and the Duty Year 1937–1939
“Why did the Four-Year Plan force the regime to abandon its domestic ideology?”

By the late 1930s, Nazi domestic ideals collided with the Four Year Plan for rearmament. Rapid military rearmament under the Four-Year Plan created acute labour shortages as men were conscripted, so munitions factories urgently needed women back on production lines. In 1937, marriage-loan wives were allowed back into factory jobs, and in 1938 young unmarried women faced a compulsory Duty Year in farming or textiles. As a result, female employment went up, rising from 11.6 million to 14.6 million (37% of Germany's workforce) by May 1939.

KEY ENQUIRY “How did the Nazis use the education system and youth movements to indoctrinate the next generation, and how successful were they?”
STAGE 1 The Hitler Youth (Hitlerjugend): Military Drill and Battlefield Preparation 1933–1939
“How did the Hitlerjugend train German boys for war and obedience?”

Under Reich Youth Leader Baldur von Schirach, life for boys aged 14–18 centred on the Hitlerjugend. Governed by the Law for the Hitler Youth, it toughened boys into fearless soldiers through obstacle courses, boxing, and rifle shooting rather than classroom study. Enrolling over 8 million young Germans overall by 1939, weekend camps drilled boys in route marches with heavy packs, trench map reading, and signalling. After passing fitness tests, boys received engraved Hitler Youth daggers stamped Blood and Honour, cementing personal loyalty to Hitler.

STAGE 2 The League of German Girls: Bund Deutscher Mädel and Motherhood Training 1933–1939
“How did the BDM train adolescent girls for motherhood and domestic duty?”

Younger boys aged 10–14 entered the Deutsches Jungvolk (German Young People), while teenage girls aged 14–18 joined the Bund Deutscher Mädel (BDM or League of German Girls). Wearing dark blue skirts and white blouses, girls practised gymnastics, swimming, and long-distance running to become healthy, racially pure mothers. BDM meetings focused on domestic training — cooking, meal planning, needlework, and infant care — alongside racial science (Rassenkunde) instruction. Girls studied family charts to pick Aryan husbands, shun racial intermarriage, and sing folk songs celebrating the Volksgemeinschaft.

Nazi Policies Towards the Young — Escalation & Consequences

Stages 3–5
STAGE 3 Compulsory Indoctrination: The 1936 Youth Law and Banning Independent Clubs 1933–1939
“Why did the regime make youth group attendance legally compulsory in 1939?”

To win total control over young minds, the Nazi state dismantled rival youth clubs. Independent scouts, sports teams, and church youth leagues were systematically banned after Gestapo pressure so the Hitler Youth (Hitlerjugend, HJ) stood alone. In December 1936, the Hitler Youth Act gave Schirach sole charge of youth training outside school. A second decree in March 1939 enforced compulsory membership for all eligible German 10-to-18-year-olds. Although millions enjoyed campfires and outdoor comradeship, rebellious teenagers formed dissident groups like the Edelweiss Pirates to escape constant military drill.

STAGE 4 Purging the Staffroom: The National Socialist Teachers' League 1933–1939
“How did the regime ensure that school teachers actively delivered propaganda?”

Turning German schools into ideological training grounds required tight control over staff. Under the April 1933 Civil Service Law, Jewish, socialist, and democratic teachers were sacked. Remaining educators had to join the National Socialist Teachers' League (NSLB), enrolling 97% of all German teachers by 1937. The NSLB ran mandatory ideological summer camps during holidays, drilling teachers in physical fitness and Nazi racial biology. Every school lesson began and ended with the Nazi salute, and teachers lacking Nazi zeal risked denunciation by pupils and dismissal.

STAGE 5 The Nazified Curriculum: Racial Biology and the Napolas Elite Cadet Schools 1933–1939
“How were academic subjects rewritten to reinforce racial theory and militarism?”

Every school curriculum was rewritten by 1939 to serve war preparation and racial hatred. New history textbooks portrayed the past as an endless struggle against Jewish enemies. Meanwhile, school maths problems practised calculating bombing trajectories and the financial cost of caring for the disabled. Compulsory lessons in racial biology and Eugenics taught children to measure skull shapes and rank Aryan "masters" above "subhumans". Meanwhile, elite SS-backed boarding academies called Napolas (National Political Educational Institutes) put selected boys through harsh military training to produce ruthless future commanders.

KEY ENQUIRY “How did the Nazis achieve the 'economic miracle' of full employment, and did the standard of living actually improve for German workers?”
STAGE 1 Smashed Unions: Robert Ley and the German Labour Front (DAF) May 1933
“How did the DAF destroy independent collective bargaining for wages?”

Alongside expanding the National Labour Service, Hitler moved swiftly to crush organized labour on 2 May 1933. SA and SS stormtroopers raided trade union offices nationwide, jailed union officials, and abolished independent unions, while Dr Robert Ley replaced them with the German Labour Front (DAF). Absorbing both workers and employers into one state body, the DAF banned strikes and froze wages. In 1935, compulsory workbooks (Arbeitsbuch) stopped workers changing jobs without written permission, while compulsory DAF dues (plus 5 marks weekly for car savings) tied workers to arms factories.

STAGE 2 The Labour Army: Konstantin Hierl and the Reich Labour Service (RAD) 1933–1935
“How did compulsory RAD service and Autobahn works cut unemployment?”

Inheriting over six million registered unemployed workers in 1933, Nazi work schemes cut official figures to under 300,000 unemployed workers by 1939. Under Konstantin Hierl, the Reich Labour Service (Reichsarbeitsdienst or RAD) became compulsory in July 1935, requiring six months' labour from unmarried men aged 18–25. Living in camp barracks, RAD conscripts drilled in uniform with spades instead of rifles. Workers drained peat marshes, planted forests, and carried out Autobahn highway construction across Germany, instilling military discipline for tiny pay.

Employment and Living Standards — Escalation & Consequences

Stages 3–5
STAGE 3 Rearmament and Conscription: The Reality of 'Invisible Unemployment' 1935–1939
“Why was the Nazi claim of full employment statistically misleading?”

Nazi propaganda hailed an "economic miracle of full employment" while preparing the nation for war, as jobless counts fell from six million in 1933 to under 300,000 by 1939. Reintroducing military conscription in March 1935 drafted 500,000 men at first — absorbing 1.4m men into the Wehrmacht by 1939 — alongside booming arms orders. Yet historians show this statistical manipulation masked Invisible Unemployment. Dismissing Jews and married women from civil service, medical, and teaching jobs excluded them from jobless counts. RAD recruits and camp prisoners were omitted too to mask real jobless numbers.

STAGE 4 Strength Through Joy (KdF): Subsidized Leisure and the People's Car 1933–1939
“How did leisure schemes compensate workers for lost political rights?”

To reconcile workers to long hours after abolishing Trade Unions, Robert Ley set up Strength Through Joy (Kraft durch Freude, KdF) inside the German Labour Front (DAF). Subsidised by the state, KdF ran cheap concerts, theatre trips, sports, hikes, and luxury cruise voyages to Norway and the Mediterranean. KdF also promoted the Volkswagen savings scheme, letting workers pay five marks a week toward a "People's Car". However, no worker ever received a civilian car; when war broke out in 1939, factories switched to army vehicles and workers' savings were seized for rearmament.

STAGE 5 Guns vs Butter: The Four-Year Plan and Falling Living Standards 1936–1939
“How did total rearmament erode real worker purchasing power by 1939?”

Alongside 6 months of RAD service, Hermann Göring took command of the Four-Year Plan in 1936 to prepare the German economy for war within four years. Göring pursued Autarky (economic self-sufficiency), pouring billions into costly domestic synthetic rubber (Buna), synthetic oil from coal, and low-grade iron ore. Choosing military "guns before butter" strained civilian living standards with food rationing and shortages of butter, meat, and consumer goods. Average working weeks rose from 43 to 49 hours by 1939 while real wages barely matched 1928 levels.

KEY ENQUIRY “How and why did the Nazis persecute minorities, and how did the persecution of Jewish people escalate between 1933 and 1939?”
STAGE 1 Racial Hierarchy: Aryan 'Herrenvolk' Ideology and the April Boycott 1933
“How did the regime initiate public state-sponsored antisemitic discrimination?”

Driven by fanatical Social Darwinism and biological racism, Nazi policy placed Nordic Aryan Herrenvolk ("master race") Germans at the peak of a racial hierarchy. Propaganda branded Jews, Roma, and Slavs as Untermenschen ("subhumans") threatening to poison German blood and block national rebirth. State persecution began on 1 April 1933 with a one-day boycott of Jewish shops, law firms, and clinics. Armed SA men painted yellow Stars of David on windows, and six days later the Civil Service Act purged Jewish civil servants, judges, and teachers.

STAGE 2 The Nuremberg Laws: Reich Citizenship and the Protection of German Blood Sep 1935
“Why did the Nuremberg Laws turn antisemitism into legal statute?”

At the September 1935 Nuremberg rally, Hitler codified racial persecution into law through two Nuremberg Laws. First, the Reich Citizenship Law stripped Jewish people of their German citizenship and the right to vote or hold office. Overnight, German Jews became stateless subjects in their own homeland. Second, the Law for the Protection of German Blood outlawed marriage and sexual relations between Jews and Aryans as criminal "racial defilement". Crucially, Jewish identity was defined by ancestry rather than faith: anyone with three or four Jewish grandparents counted legally as Jewish.

The Persecution of Minorities — Escalation & Consequences

Stages 3–5
STAGE 3 Biological Purity: Persecuting Roma, Homosexuals and the Disabled 1933–1939
“How did forced sterilisation and the 1939 T4 program target minorities?”

Grounded in Social Darwinism, the Nazi racial state persecuted other minorities deemed "unfit". Hundreds of Roma families were rounded up into enclosed municipal internment camps like Marzahn before deportation to concentration camps. Under Paragraph 175, over 15,000 gay men were jailed in camps wearing pink triangles. Disabled and mentally ill citizens suffered murderous persecution too. After 400,000 forced sterilisations under the 1933 law, Hitler secretly authorised the Euthanasia T4 program in 1939. Doctors murdered disabled children and adults using lethal injections and carbon monoxide gas chambers.

STAGE 4 Kristallnacht: The Night of Broken Glass and State-Sponsored Terror Nov 1938
“How did the November pogrom mark a decisive shift toward violent state terror?”

On 9–10 November 1938, persecution escalated from legal discrimination into state-orchestrated terror during Kristallnacht (The Night of Broken Glass). After a young Polish Jew shot German diplomat vom Rath in Paris, Joseph Goebbels unleashed plainclothes SA and SS mobs nationwide while police and fire crews stood back. Across the Reich, over 1,000 synagogues burned, 7,500 Jewish shops were smashed and looted, and 91 Jews were murdered. In addition, 30,000 Jewish men were rounded up and sent to concentration camps at Dachau, Buchenwald, and Sachsenhausen to force families to emigrate.

STAGE 5 Economic Destruction: Aryanisation, Collective Fines and Forced Flight 1938–1939
“How did the regime eliminate all remaining Jewish participation in economic life?”

Following the pogrom of 9-10 November 1938, Göring blamed the Jewish community for the damage, seized their insurance payouts, and imposed a 1 billion mark fine. New decrees barred Jews from public parks, cinemas, theatres, and swimming pools, and expelled Jewish children from state schools. Under forced "Aryanisation" — the forced transfer of Jewish businesses and property to Aryans at rock-bottom prices — remaining Jewish shops and factories were seized. Meanwhile, Heydrich’s Reich Office for Jewish Emigration stripped emigrants' assets as over half of Germany’s Jews fled before September 1939.
