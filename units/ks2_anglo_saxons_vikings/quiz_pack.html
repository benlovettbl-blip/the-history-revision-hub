Enquiry 1: After the Romans Left — Why Did Angles, Saxons & Jutes Settle in Britain? (c. AD 410–600)
Q1. Which three seafaring peoples crossed the North Sea from Jutland, Angeln, and Saxony to settle in Britain after AD 410? [ Angles, Saxons, and Jutes / Romans, Greeks, and Egyptians ]
Q2. What was a key "push reason" driving families to leave their North Sea coastal homelands in the 5th century?
Q3. In an early Anglo-Saxon settlement like West Stow, who was the "cyning" and who were the "ceorls"?
Q4. What name do historians give to the seven main Anglo-Saxon kingdoms (Northumbria, Mercia, East Anglia, Essex, Kent, Sussex, and Wessex)?
Enquiry 2: Ship Burials & Monasteries — What Do Sutton Hoo & Christian Monks Reveal? (c. AD 597–750)
Q5. Even though acidic Suffolk sand rotted the oak wood and bones in Mound 1 at Sutton Hoo, how did Basil Brown prove a 27-metre ship was buried there in 1939? [ Rows of rusted iron rivets and dark stained sand still preserved the exact shape of the hull / A Roman photograph was buried inside a glass jar ]
Q6. Where had the glowing red garnets set into Sutton Hoo’s gold shoulder clasps and sword fittings travelled from?
Q7. Who arrived at Canterbury in AD 597 from Rome, and who founded Lindisfarne Priory in Northumbria in AD 635?
Q8. What smooth material were precious illuminated manuscripts like the Lindisfarne Gospels painted onto using feather quills?
Enquiry 3: Fury of the Northmen — Why Did Vikings Raid Lindisfarne & Conquer Kingdoms? (AD 793–878)
Q9. Why were coastal monasteries like Lindisfarne (raided on 8 June AD 793) considered tempting "soft targets" by early Viking crews? [ They stood isolated by the sea packed with silver chalices and gold reliquaries, guarded only by unarmed monks / They had giant Roman catapults on the roof ]
Q10. How did a clinker-built Viking longship’s "1-metre shallow draught" help warriors attack inland towns?
Q11. What natural material soaked in pine tar did Norse shipwrights press between overlapping oak strakes to make longships watertight?
Q12. How did Viking attacks change dramatically in AD 865 when the Great Heathen Army landed under Ivar the Boneless, Halfdan, and Guthrum?

Enquiry 4: Alfred the Great, Burhs & the Danelaw — How Did Wessex Survive? (AD 871–899)
Q13. Where did King Alfred hide with a small band of followers after Guthrum's surprise Twelfth Night attack on Chippenham in January AD 878? [ Inside the misty Somerset marshes on the Isle of Athelney / Aboard a ship sailing to Rome ]
Q14. At which May AD 878 battle in Wiltshire did King Alfred's locked shield wall decisively defeat Viking leader Guthrum?
Q15. What happened under the peace treaty signed after Edington between King Alfred and Guthrum?
Q16. How did King Alfred's network of 33 fortified "burhs" (such as Wareham and Wallingford) protect families across Wessex?
Enquiry 5: Life in Jorvik & the Danelaw — Were the Vikings Only Fierce Raiders? (c. AD 876–1000)
Q17. Why did 40,000 wooden, leather, bone, and fabric objects survive underground beneath Coppergate in York (Viking Jorvik)? [ Deep, wet, oxygen-free peaty soil stopped bacteria from rotting organic materials away / They were sealed inside solid glass museum cases by King Guthrum ]
Q18. What does the York street name "Coppergate" actually mean in Old Norse — and what did archaeologists find on Source A (the Coppergate Helmet)?
Q19. Which finds from Jorvik and Viking hoards prove that Viking trade networks reached the Middle East and the Red Sea?
Q20. In Danelaw Viking society, who wore heavy iron household keys on their belts, could own land, and ran farms while husbands sailed?
Enquiry 6: Aethelflaed, Athelstan, Cnut & 1066 — Who Won the Struggle for England? (AD 900–1066)
Q21. Which bold daughter of King Alfred ruled as "Lady of the Mercians" (AD 911–918), building burhs at Tamworth and Warwick and capturing Viking Derby and Leicester? [ Aethelflaed / Queen Boudicca ]
Q22. Why do historians recognise King Athelstan (reigned AD 924–939) as the first true "King of All England"?
Q23. What was "Danegeld," and how was Danish King Cnut's rule (AD 1016–1035) different from earlier raids?
Q24. Why did a three-way crisis erupt over the English throne in January 1066?
